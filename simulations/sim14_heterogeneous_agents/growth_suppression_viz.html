Balance Ratio vs n

The balance ratio = √n × α × (1−fill) / (β × √fill) crosses 1.0 at n≈3000 (the 30th ceiling). Below 1.0: stable (suppression > growth). Above 1.0: unstable (growth > suppression). The ratio is non-monotonic — it drops at high n because the grid fills up (logistic carrying capacity), but the system stays unstable because the phase transition is irreversible.

Fill Cap Trajectory

Fill cap (with boundary) follows a stretched exponential fill = 0.31 × exp(0.006 × √n) (R²=0.995). The no-inhibition control fills ~100%. The 30th ceiling (red dashed) marks where stable composition collapses.

Gelimson & Golestanian mapping: θ = (μα₀ + λ)C₀. Their λ (growth) ↔ our α×n (extensive, deposit rate × n_termites). Their μα₀ (chemotactic suppression) ↔ our β×√n (intensive, diffusion length). Their C₀ (carrying capacity) ↔ our grid² (25600 cells). Their threshold μ < λ/α₀ ↔ our √n > β/α ↔ n > (β/α)². Their separatrix ↔ our 30th ceiling. Their irreversibility (flow to strong-coupling fixed point) ↔ our permanent loss of boundary coherence.

Non-monotonicity: The ratio rises from 0.74 (n=1200) to 1.13 (n=5000), then drops to 0.51 (n=10000). The drop is from the (1−fill) factor shrinking as the grid fills — the logistic carrying capacity effect. The system stays unstable because the phase transition is irreversible: once the boundary loses coherence, reducing the growth rate does not restore it. This is the spatial analog of G&G's non-accessible strong-coupling fixed point.
