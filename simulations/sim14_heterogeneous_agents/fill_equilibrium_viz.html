The finding

The fill equilibrium follows a power law fill ∝ n0.28 / √decay, not the naive prediction fill ∝ n / decay. The n-exponent (0.28 < 1) means the fill grows sublinearly — the focal bias's concentration weakens with n. The decay-exponent (−0.50, not −1.00) means the deposit rate increases as the structure shrinks — a smaller structure has higher surface-to-volume ratio, giving agents higher curvature → higher deposit probability. The self-consistent formula with 1/√A surface scaling fails (R² = −0.27): the actual morphology's surface scaling is more complex than a disk's.

Self-consistent formula (failed)

The self-consistent formula (deposit rate = p_base + K/√(fill×grid²), solved iteratively) gave R² = −0.27 — worse than the mean. The 1/√A surface-to-volume scaling assumes a disk-like morphology, but the curvature channel produces complex branched structures with different surface scaling. The power law (empirical) outperforms the self-consistent formula (theoretical) because the theoretical surface scaling is wrong.
