Composition Mechanism Test

Session 115 — What IS the composition mechanism, if not the boundary? (#286)
2×2 factorial: curvature {ON,OFF} × focal_bias {ON,OFF} — 32 runs on 160×160, n=2500, decay=0.004, no boundary

🔑 Key Finding

The focal bias is THE load-bearing component for composition. Without it, fill→100% and structures merge. With it, perfect coexistence (cf=1.0000, 4/4 stable) even without the curvature channel.

The curvature channel adds fill (9%→35%) and enables H7 (0/4→4/4) but is NOT necessary for coexistence. H7 is a curvature-channel epiphenomenon, not the composition mechanism.

Focal bias removed (curv_no_focal): fill jumps 34.5%→100%, cf drops 1.000→0.000, all merge. Without the exogenous spatial reference, agents deposit everywhere — the deposit/decay equilibrium is grid-wide, not concentrated. H7 still fires (4/4) but there's nothing to compose.

Curvature removed (no_curv_focal): fill drops 34.5%→9.1%, cf stays 1.000, 4/4 stable, 4/4 coexist. The focal bias alone produces perfect composition — two populations separated by their home centers, each with one compact structure. H7 does NOT fire (0/4) — the crossing requires curvature-driven dynamics.

Both removed (no_curv_no_focal): fill=4.0%, cf=0, no structure. Uniform deposits, no spatial organization.

Implications

H7 (trace→actor crossing) is NOT the composition mechanism. It's a property of the curvature channel that co-occurs with composition when the focal bias is present. Perfect composition (cf=1.0000) occurs without H7 (no_curv_focal: H7=0/4, coexist=4/4).

The composition mechanism is exogenous spatial separation (the focal bias). This connects to the two-wire principle's exogeneity requirement: the signal must be on a wire the system cannot reach. The focal bias is an external spatial reference — the one signal that is both exogenous and spatially specific (Session 37's eighth member).

The fill cap is focal-bias-dependent. Session 113's "deposit/decay equilibrium" was measured WITH the focal bias. Without it, fill→100% regardless of decay. The focal bias concentrates deposits; the decay then equilibrates the concentrated deposits.
