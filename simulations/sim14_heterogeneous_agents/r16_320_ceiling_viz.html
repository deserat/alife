Why the r/grid Scaling Fails

The boundary's suppression depends on TWO components:
1. Spatial reach (r/grid) — r=16 gives 5% of grid, matching 160×160
2. Signal strength (b_scale) — the co-presence signal is 4× sparser on 320×320

The r/grid ratio captures (1) but not (2). The co-presence signal is intensive — it depends on the spatial arrangement of material, not just the dilation radius. On a 4× larger grid, the same r=16 (5% of grid) reaches far enough, but the signal it encounters is too sparse to generate sufficient boundary suppression. The boundary's fill-capping power is ~56% on 160×160 but ~0% on 320×320 r=16.

This is the 53rd mechanism (extensive/intensive distinction) applied to the 30th ceiling: the ceiling depends on the boundary's effective suppression, which has both a spatial (extensive, r) and a density (intensive, b_scale) component. r/grid captures the spatial component but misses the density component.
