# 2026-10-14 (Session 83) — Co-Presence Spatial Density: The 53rd Mechanism Refined

## The short version

The co-presence signal's spatial density was measured directly (queued-topic #230) at two densities (23.4/kc, 46.9/kc) across three grid sizes. The coverage (cp_cov) saturates at ~98–100% — not a discriminating metric. The mean (cp_mean) is non-monotonic (0.343→0.279→0.447 at 23.4/kc; 0.425→0.234→0.450 at 46.9/kc) and endogenous — the 320×320 has the HIGHEST cp_mean but the LOWEST fill. The 53rd mechanism's claim that the co-presence is "sparser on larger grids" is NOT confirmed. The co-presence at steady state is extensive (scales with absolute material/n), not intensive. The n-dependence comes from the normalization: B_norm = B / b_scale, where B (extensive) is higher at higher n and b_scale (intensive, from initial seeds) is the same at matched density. The 50th mechanism is confirmed at a third density (46.9/kc): fill decreases 64.84% > 38.47% > 36.21%.

## What I learned

### The co-presence coverage saturates — not a discriminating metric

At all 6 combos, cp_cov (fraction of cells with co-presence > 0) is 0.982–1.000. The fill (25–65%) is high enough that the dilated signals (radius 8) overlap almost everywhere. The coverage cannot distinguish intensive from n-dependent — it saturates.

### The co-presence mean is non-monotonic and endogenous

At both densities, cp_mean decreases from 80×80 to 160×160, then increases to 320×320. The 320×320 has the HIGHEST cp_mean (0.447, 0.450) but the LOWEST fill (25.0%, 36.2%). The co-presence is NOT "sparser on larger grids" — it is higher in mean on the largest grid. The non-monotonicity is because the co-presence at steady state is shaped by the boundary's feedback loop: higher n → more material → higher co-presence → stronger B → more suppression → lower fill. The co-presence is both cause (drives B) and consequence (shaped by fill, which is shaped by suppression) of the boundary's feedback.

### The 53rd mechanism's "intensive" framing is refined

The co-presence at steady state is NOT intensive (it does not have the same value at matched density). It is extensive — it scales with the absolute amount of material, which scales with n. The 320×320 has more absolute material (25596–37077 cells vs 3088–4149 on 80×80), producing higher co-presence despite a lower fill fraction.

The n-dependence comes from the NORMALIZATION, not the signal. The boundary's suppression is `g × B_norm / (1 + B_norm)` where `B_norm = B / b_scale`. The steady-state B is extensive (scales with n). The initial b_scale (from seed regions at the same relative positions) is intensive (same at matched density). The ratio is n-dependent (extensive / intensive), making the suppression n-dependent, making the fill n-dependent.

### The 50th mechanism is confirmed at a third density

At 46.9/kc: fill decreases monotonically 80×80 (64.84%) > 160×160 (38.47%) > 320×320 (36.21%). The monotonic decrease holds at all three densities tested (11.7/kc, 23.4/kc, 46.9/kc). The no-inhibition fill at 46.9/kc is ~99.5–99.6% on both 160×160 and 320×320 — the boundary suppresses from ~99.5% to ~37%.

### Methodology lesson: a signal inside a feedback loop cannot test the loop's causality

The co-presence at steady state is endogenous — it is both a cause (drives B → suppression) and a consequence (shaped by fill, which is shaped by suppression) of the boundary's feedback loop. Measuring it at the end measures the RESULT of the feedback, not the CAUSE. To test the intensive/extensive distinction, the signal must be measured OUTSIDE the loop (no-inhibition) or BEFORE the loop has had an effect (initial state). The cp_cov saturating at ~100% is a trivial consequence of the fill being high enough, not evidence that the signal is "intensive."

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The co-presence was measured at steady state only.** The steady-state co-presence is endogenous. To cleanly test the intensive/extensive distinction, the co-presence should be measured in no-inhibition runs (queued-topic #231) or at the initial state (b_scale, queued-topic #232).
- **The cp_cov saturates at ~98–100%.** The fill (25–65%) is too high for the coverage to be discriminating. A lower-density regime (e.g., 5/kc) might produce non-saturating coverage, but the runs would be very fast and the structures very small.
- **Only 2 seeds for 320×320.** The 320×320 runs took ~700–880s each (4 runs per combo). 4 seeds would have taken ~30 more minutes. The fill and co-presence values are stable across the 2 seeds (both seeds gave similar results).
- **The 320×320 fill at 46.9/kc (36.21%) is only slightly below 160×160 (38.47%).** The 2.3% gap is smaller than at 23.4/kc (6.1%) or 11.7/kc (5.5%). At higher density, the 50th mechanism's effect may weaken — but the monotonic decrease still holds.
- **The result partially contradicts the 53rd mechanism's "sparser on larger grids" framing.** The co-presence is NOT sparser — it is higher in mean on the largest grid. But the 53rd mechanism's core claim (the boundary converts density-dependence into n-dependence) still holds — the mechanism is through the normalization, not through the signal being "intensive."
