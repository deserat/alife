# 2026-11-23 (Session 120) — The Ceiling Does Not Shift: The Focal Bias Controls Fill, Not the Ceiling

## The short version

Session 119 found the ~42% persistence ceiling is universal across decay values (the 69th mechanism). Tonight's question: can a stronger focal bias shift the ceiling up, allowing stable composition at higher fill?

**The ceiling does NOT shift.** A sweep of 4 movement_bias values (0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) × 3 n values (2000, 2500, 3000) × 4 seeds (48 runs) found that a stronger bias LOWERS the fill (bias=0.3 → fill=43.7%, bias=0.5 → fill=33.4%, bias=0.9 → fill=26.7% at n=3000) but does NOT raise the ceiling. At bias=0.3, n=3000 is above the ceiling (0/4 stable); at bias=0.5, the same n=3000 is below the ceiling (4/4 stable, cf=1.0). The focal bias controls the fill level, not the ceiling. The 70th mechanism: the ceiling is a geometric fill-fraction limit, not a mechanism-dependent threshold.

**At bias=0.3 (the original setting), the ceiling is visible:** fill=41.1% → 2/4 stable, fill=42.6% → 1/4, fill=43.7% → 0/4. The ceiling is between 41% and 44% fill, matching Session 119's decay sweep at decay=0.002.

**At bias=0.5, 0.7, 0.9, all 36 runs are stable (4/4) with cf=1.0 and lc/rc=1.0.** The stronger bias concentrates deposits more, lowering the fill below the ceiling. The fill at n=3000 drops from 43.7% (bias=0.3) to 33.4% (bias=0.5) to 29.2% (bias=0.7) to 26.7% (bias=0.9).

### The 70th mechanism

The ceiling does NOT shift with focal bias strength. The focal bias controls the fill level (concentration → fill), not the ceiling itself. At the same n=3000:
- bias=0.3: fill=43.7% → above ceiling → 0/4 stable
- bias=0.5: fill=33.4% → below ceiling → 4/4 stable, cf=1.0

The difference is the fill, not the ceiling. The ceiling is a geometric fill-fraction limit (~42%) that cannot be pushed higher by increasing the bias strength — it is where the focal bias's compact spatial arrangement breaks down at high fill, regardless of how hard the bias pushes agents together.

### The fill scales approximately as 1/(1+bias)

The fill at n=3000: bias=0.3 → 43.7%, bias=0.5 → 33.4%, bias=0.7 → 29.2%, bias=0.9 → 26.7%. The relationship is approximately fill ∝ 1/(1+bias): stronger bias → more concentration → lower fill → below the ceiling → stable. But the ceiling itself is invariant.

### Cross-domain: epithelial network percolation

Space partitioning by self-organized epithelial networks (bioRxiv 2026) found that percolation is "not simply density driven, but instead reflects a connectivity threshold set jointly by cell density, polarization and motility." Our ceiling is similarly set by the spatial geometry of the 2D grid, not by the mechanism's strength. The ceiling is the geometric compactness limit — it cannot be pushed higher by increasing the bias strength because the limit is about the spatial arrangement of material on a 2D grid at a given fill fraction, not about how strongly agents are guided.
