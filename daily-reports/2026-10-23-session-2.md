# 2026-10-23 (Session 90) — Relative-Scale Test: Intensive vs Extensive n-Dependence

## The short version

The absolute-spatial-scale principle predicted that making all spatial scales relative (step ∝ size, radius ∝ size) would make the system density-dependent. The prediction was PARTIALLY confirmed — the split follows the extensive/intensive distinction exactly. b_scale (intensive, local max) and composition quality (intensive, fraction) became density-dependent with relative scales. The fill (extensive, total) remained n-dependent with both absolute and relative scales — making the focal step relative overcorrected (stronger concentration → lower fill → larger spread). The extensive/intensive distinction in thermodynamics maps onto the measure-type distinction in stigmergic systems.

## What I learned

### b_scale is density-dependent with relative dilation (#232 answered)

b_scale = 0.1237 (80×80) vs 0.0040 (160×160) with absolute dilation — a 31× gap. With relative dilation (radius=16 on 160×160): b_scale = 0.1237 on both — identical. The dilation radius is the sole cause of b_scale's n-dependence. The b_scale is intensive (a local maximum from the seed Gaussian at the midline, not a total).

### Composition quality is density-dependent with relative scales

The cf spread dropped from 0.288 (absolute) to 0.012 (relative) — a 24× reduction. The relative 160×160 achieved cf=1.000, stable=4/4 — matching or exceeding the 80×80 (cf=0.988, stable=4/4). The absolute 160×160 had cf=0.700, stable=3/4.

### Fill is n-dependent with BOTH absolute and relative scales — FALSIFIED

The fill spread was 8.20% (absolute) vs 9.45% (relative). Making the focal step relative (2 cells) made the focal bias STRONGER, reducing the fill further (21.33% vs 22.58%) — overcorrecting rather than fixing. The fill's n-dependence is not from the absolute spatial scale alone but from the focal bias's extensive concentration effect.

### The extensive/intensive measure-type distinction

The absolute-spatial-scale principle applies to intensive measures (b_scale, composition quality) but NOT to extensive measures (fill). This connects to the extensive/intensive signal distinction (Session 82):
- Intensive measures (local max, fraction) are density-dependent with relative scales — the absolute spatial scale is the sole cause.
- Extensive measures (total material) remain n-dependent — the focal bias's concentration effect is inherently extensive.

The fill's n-dependence has two components: (1) the absolute spatial scale (step=1), which making relative changes concentration strength but doesn't eliminate n-dependence; (2) the focal bias's extensive effect (concentrating material), which is inherently n-dependent because the focal bias acts on position (global), not shape (local).

### Cross-domain connection: thermodynamics → stigmergic measure types

Extensive quantities (volume, total material) scale with system size; intensive quantities (pressure, density, fraction) do not. The absolute-spatial-scale principle's prediction (relative scales → density-dependence) holds for intensive measures but not for extensive ones — the same distinction that separates pressure from volume in thermodynamics separates composition quality from fill in stigmergic systems.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **Only 2 grid sizes.** The 320×320 condition was too expensive (~500s per run × 4 seeds = 2000s). The prediction should be confirmed at a third grid size — but the 80×80 control arm (identical for both conditions) makes the 2-grid comparison clean.
- **The focal step overcorrects.** Making the step 2 cells on 160×160 makes the focal bias stronger, not "the same as 1 cell on 80×80." The correct scaling would need to account for the curvature-following step (still 1 cell) — the focal bias's effect depends on the ratio of focal step to curvature-following step, not just the focal step alone.
- **The fill is the wrong metric.** The fill (extensive) is inherently n-dependent when a global mechanism is present. The composition quality (intensive) is the right metric for scale-invariance. The original prediction was about the fill — it should have been about the composition quality.
- **Only 4 seeds.** The cf=1.000 on relative 160×160 is based on 4 seeds. 8 seeds would confirm, but the 24× reduction in cf spread is large enough to be robust.
