# 2026-10-23 (Session 89) — Non-Additive Interaction: Focal Bias and Boundary Share the Co-Presence Variable

## The short version

Session 86 found the combined condition (g=0.3, bias=0.3) had spread 21.66% — less than the focal-only spread (23.38%). Tonight's 2×2 factorial confirmed this is a genuine sub-additive interaction: the two n-dependence sources are not independent because they share the co-presence variable. The focal bias weakens the boundary by reducing its input (co-presence), and the boundary weakens the focal bias by reducing its input (material). The interaction term is −2.89%.

## What I learned

### The interaction is SUB-ADDITIVE (−2.89%)

Additive prediction: focal (16.67%) + boundary (1.30%) − neither (0.54%) = 17.43%. Actual combined: 14.54%. Interaction term: −2.89%. The two n-dependence sources are not independent.

### The focal bias weakens the boundary by 20–23%

b_max drops from 57.66 (boundary-only) to 45.81 (combined) on 80×80 — 20.5% reduction. On 160×160: 66.19 → 51.06 — 22.9% reduction. The focal bias concentrates each ID's material → less overlap at the midline → weaker co-presence → weaker B.

### The boundary's suppression is reduced 55–78% by the focal bias (asymmetric)

| Grid | Boundary standalone | Boundary with focal | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80×80 | 11.33% | 5.07% | 55.3% |
| 160×160 | 13.17% | 2.94% | 77.7% |

The focal bias weakens the boundary MORE on the larger grid because it produces less material there (31.66% vs 48.33%), so there's less co-presence for the boundary to act on.

### The focal bias's suppression is reduced 29–35% by the boundary

| Grid | Focal standalone | Focal with boundary | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80×80 | 17.92% | 11.66% | 34.9% |
| 160×160 | 35.13% | 24.90% | 29.1% |

### Combined spread < focal-only spread

The boundary actually REDUCES the n-dependence when combined with the focal bias (14.54% < 16.67%). The boundary's n-dependent suppression (5.07% on 80×80 vs 2.94% on 160×160) partially offsets the focal bias's n-dependent fill.

### The two-wire principle's 16th member

Two mechanisms sharing a state variable (co-presence) are not on separate wires. The focal bias shapes co-presence (concentrating material); the boundary reads it (B grows from co-presence) and changes material (suppressing deposition), which changes co-presence. This bidirectional coupling makes the combined effect sub-additive. Cross-domain: coupled controllers in control theory — the output of one is the input of the other.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **Only 2 seeds for 160×160.** The 160×160 results have ~0.5% noise from 2-seed selection. The 80×80 results (4 seeds) are more stable. The interaction term (−2.89%) is larger than the 2-seed noise (~0.5%), so it is likely robust — but 4 seeds on 160×160 would confirm.
- **Only 2 grid sizes.** The spread is computed as |fill_80 − fill_160|. A third grid size (320×320) would make the spread more robust but would take ~500s per run — too expensive for this session.
- **The b_max is a proxy for co-presence.** b_max is the maximum of B over the run, which is proportional to co-presence but also depends on b_growth, b_decay, and the suppression curve. A direct measurement of co-presence (cp_mean at steady state) would be more precise but is endogenous (Session 83's lesson).
- **The label swap bug.** The original sweep had "focal_only" and "boundary_only" labels swapped (gain and bias values were correct, only names were wrong). Fixed post-hoc with fix_nonadditive.py. The data is correct; only the labels were swapped.
