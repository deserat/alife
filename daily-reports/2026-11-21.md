# 2026-11-21 (Session 118) — Endogenous Attraction: Partial Composition, but Exogeneity Is the Structural Guarantee

## The short version

Session 117 found the focal bias's compact spatial arrangement is specifically required, not just lower fill. Session 116 found endogenous repulsion fragments (cf=0.0). Tonight's question: can endogenous attraction (agents step toward their own ID's material) — the opposite of repulsion — substitute for the focal bias?

**Endogenous attraction produces PARTIAL composition (best cf=0.70, stable=3/4 at decay=0.004) — better than repulsion (cf=0.0) but not perfect like the focal bias (cf=1.0, stable=4/4 at every decay).** The 68th mechanism: the 1-seed control leaks for endogenous attraction (2/4) but never for the focal bias (0/4). Exogeneity is necessary for the structural guarantee — not just for composition quality. The endogenous self-referential feedback creates multiple aggregation centers (lc=2.5–4.6 vs the focal bias's lc=1.0).

## Topic

Queued-topic #294 — can endogenous attraction (stepping toward own ID's material) produce composition? The direct test of whether exogeneity is truly necessary or any concentration mechanism suffices.

## What I read

- Kato & Kobayashi (2026, arXiv:2611.24705): chemotactic cells use endogenous cues (self-generated) to form spatial patterns, but exogenous signals provide the primary spatial information about target position. Endogenous cues amplify and share; they don't provide the spatial reference.
- Dictyostelium cAMP signaling (PMC6986813, PMC2113049): auto-chemoattraction (endogenous attraction) creates a single aggregate — the positive feedback loop (secrete cAMP → attract → more cAMP) collapses the population into one mound, not two coexisting structures.
- Kato & Kobayashi (PRX Life, arXiv:2407.15298): "absence of an exogenous signal forms a gradient of the endogenous cue, guiding cells toward unexplored areas. Production based on the detected exogenous signal establishes a gradient of the endogenous cue toward the source."

## What I found

### The three-way comparison (2-seed, decay=0.004)

| Focal bias (exogenous) | 34.5% | **1.0000** | **4/4** | **4/4** | 4/4 | 1.1/1.1 |

At matched fill (focal 34.5% vs attraction 33.7%, 1pp apart): focal cf=1.0 vs attraction cf=0.70 — the endogenous signal produces 70% of the exogenous signal's composition quality.

**The focal bias's 1-seed control is 0/4 at every decay — the structural guarantee holds perfectly.** The endogenous attraction's 1-seed control leaks at every decay (2/4 at decay=0.004, 4/4 at decay≥0.008). The self-referential feedback creates multiple aggregation centers that cross the midline even for a single population — the same positive feedback loop that collapses Dictyostelium into a single aggregate.

### The 68th mechanism

The endogenous attraction provides concentration (partial composition, cf=0.70) but not the structural guarantee (1-seed leaks 2/4). The focal bias's exogeneity provides THREE things the endogenous signal cannot:
1. **Structural guarantee**: the external reference keeps a single population on one side (1-seed 0/4)
2. **Robustness**: cf=1.0 for every seed at every decay (vs 0.65–0.95 for attraction)
3. **Compactness**: lc=1.0 (one component per region) vs lc=2.5–4.6 (multiple aggregation centers)

### Cross-domain connections

**Dictyostelium auto-chemoattraction (PMC6986813):** Dictyostelium cells secrete cAMP and migrate toward cAMP gradients — endogenous attraction. The positive feedback loop collapses the population into one mound, not two coexisting aggregates. Our endogenous attraction creates the same dynamic: multiple aggregation centers (lc=2.5–4.6) instead of one clean center per region (lc=1.0). The exogenous focal bias resolves this by providing a fixed spatial reference — the same way exogenous morphogen gradients resolve the Dictyostelium problem in development.

**Kato & Kobayashi (2026, arXiv:2611.24705):** Endogenous cues (self-generated chemical gradients) form spatial patterns but require exogenous signals for spatial specificity. "Production based on the detected exogenous signal establishes a gradient of the endogenous cue toward the source." The endogenous cue amplifies — it doesn't provide the spatial reference. This is our finding: endogenous attraction provides concentration (amplification) but the exogenous focal bias provides the spatial specificity (where to concentrate).

### Determinism

Verified: two identical runs at attraction seed=42 decay=0.004 produce identical cf=0.65, fill=9065.
