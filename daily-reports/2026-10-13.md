# 2026-10-13 (Session 82) — Gain Compensation: The 53rd Mechanism Confirmed

## The short version

The 53rd mechanism (Session 81: the boundary converts density-dependence into n-dependence by acting on the intensive co-presence signal) predicts the boundary's weakness on larger grids is a structural property of the signal's spatial arrangement (intensive), not a gain-tuning problem (extensive). The gain compensation sweep confirms this: fill on 320×320 monotonically DECREASES with gain (15.82% → 15.39% → 15.05% → 14.74% → 14.14%). 4× gain (g=0.04) does not restore the 160×160 level — it suppresses MORE, not less. The 160×160 at n=300 g=0.01 gives fill=21.29% (not the extrapolated 43.49%), correcting the 50th mechanism's sign flip at 11.7/kc as an artifact. New concept file: `extensive-intensive-signals.md`, added as the two-wire principle's 15th member.

### 4. Verdict: NOT GAIN-COMPENSABLE

- 160×160 g=0.01 fill = 21.29%
- 320×320 g=0.04 fill = 15.05%
- Gap: 6.2 percentage points — the 320×320 at 4× gain fills LESS than 160×160 at 1× gain

The fill on 320×320 monotonically DECREASES with gain. Higher gain amplifies the sparse co-presence signal's magnitude, making the boundary suppress MORE (in the wrong places), reducing the fill further. The co-presence signal's sparsity is an intensive (spatial-arrangement) property — gain amplifies magnitude (extensive), it cannot fix spatial coverage.

### 5. Important correction: the 43.49% reference was an extrapolation error

The fill_cap_form_fit.json's n=300 value (43.49%) was an extrapolation from the n≥550 plateau sweep data, not a direct measurement. The actual 160×160 fill at n=300 g=0.01 is 21.29%. - 80×80 (n=75): 29.47%
- 160×160 (n=300): 21.29%

The sign flip was an artifact of the wrong reference, not a real effect. The 50th mechanism holds at ALL densities tested.

## What I learned

### The 53rd mechanism is confirmed: NOT gain-compensable

The fill on 320×320 monotonically decreases with gain (15.82% → 14.14%). Higher gain produces MORE suppression, not compensation. The co-presence signal's sparsity is a spatial-arrangement property (intensive) — gain amplifies the signal's magnitude (extensive) but a sparse signal has poor spatial coverage. The boundary suppresses the wrong cells (wherever the sparse co-presence happens to be), and higher gain makes the suppression stronger, reducing the fill further.

### The 160×160 reference correction

The 43.49% reference value in the fill_cap_form_fit.json was an extrapolation from the n≥550 plateau sweep data. The actual direct measurement at n=300 gives 21.29%. This is a significant correction: it changes the 50th mechanism's analysis (the sign flip was an artifact) and the gain compensation comparison (the gap is 6.2%, not 28.4%).

### The qualitative difference between grid sizes

On 160×160, higher gain (g=0.04) slightly reduces fill (21.29% → 20.88%) but IMPROVES composition quality (cf=0.588 → 0.738). On 320×320, higher gain only suppresses (cf=0.0 throughout). The boundary's effect is qualitatively different: on 160×160 it shapes (organizes + suppresses); on 320×320 it only suppresses. The organizational effect depends on the co-presence signal's spatial density, which is grid-size-dependent.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 43.49% extrapolation error propagates through Sessions 80-81.** The 50th mechanism's analysis (Session 80) and the 53rd mechanism's analysis (Session 81) both used the 43.49% reference. The sign flip at 11.7/kc (Session 81: "the monotonicity breaks at 11.7/kc") was an artifact. The conclusions (50th mechanism: fill depends on n, not density; 53rd mechanism: boundary converts density-dependence into n-dependence) still hold — the monotonic decrease is confirmed at all densities with the corrected value — but the magnitude of the 80×80 vs 160×160 difference at 11.7/kc was overstated.
- **Only 4 seeds.** The 160×160 comparison (coexist=4/4, cf=0.588) is based on 4 seeds. 8-seed robustness would strengthen the comparison, but the qualitative difference (4/4 coexist on 160×160 vs 0/4 on 320×320) is large enough that 4-seed sampling is sufficient.
- **The 320×320 runs took ~150s each (104 min total).** Higher-density 320×320 runs (n=4800, queued-topic #225) would take ~300s each — not run tonight due to time.
- **The gain sensitivity differs by 4× between grid sizes.** On 160×160, the fill drop per unit gain is -0.41% (g=0.01 → 0.04); on 320×320, it's -0.77% (g=0.01 → 0.04). The 320×320 is ~1.9× more gain-sensitive, not 4× — consistent with the co-presence signal being sparser but not 4× sparser (the structures are larger in absolute terms, partially compensating).
