# 2026-11-19 (Session 116) — Exogeneity Is Necessary but Not Sufficient

## The short version

Session 115 found the composition mechanism is the focal bias (exogenous spatial separation). Tonight tested whether ANY separation suffices or the focal bias's specific mechanism is required. Three conditions (curvature ON, no boundary, n=2500, decay=0.004):

- **Focal bias (exogenous + concentrating)**: cf=1.0000, stable=4/4, fill=34.5% → perfect composition
- **Region-restricted deposit (exogenous, diffuse)**: cf=0.40, stable=2/4, fill=75.7% → partial composition (above the ~44% ceiling → over-split, fragmented)
- **Endogenous repulsion (endogenous)**: cf=0.0000, stable=0/4, fill=43.5% → zero composition (fragmented, lc/rc=13.8/13.3)

Neither alternative produces the focal bias's perfect composition. The focal bias provides BOTH (1) exogeneity (external reference the system can't reach) AND (2) concentration (stepping toward a center → compact deposits → low fill → below the ceiling). Region-restricted deposit provides exogeneity but not concentration (deposits spread diffusely, fill 75%). Endogenous repulsion provides neither — it's endogenous and actively fragments. The two-wire principle's eighth member refined: **exogeneity is necessary but not sufficient; the Heisenberg trade-off (Session 40) requires both exogeneity AND spatial specificity (compact deposits).**

## What this means

1. **The focal bias's specific combination of exogeneity + concentration is the composition mechanism.** Neither alternative produces perfect composition. The focal bias provides BOTH (1) exogeneity (external reference the system can't reach — the two-wire principle's eighth member) AND (2) concentration (stepping toward a center → compact deposits → low fill → below the ~44% ceiling).

2. **Region-restricted deposit (exogenous, diffuse) is not sufficient.** The deposits spread diffusely across the half (fill 75.7%, above the ~44% ceiling → over-splitting, lc/rc=2.9/2.2). Exogeneity alone is not sufficient — concentration (compact deposits) is also required. cf=0.40 is partial composition, not the perfect cf=1.0 the focal bias achieves.

3. **Endogenous repulsion actively fragments.** The repulsion (stepping away from other ID's material) shatters the structures (lc/rc=13.8/13.3) instead of separating them compactly. The endogenous signal creates a feedback loop: agents avoid other-ID material → create gaps → the gaps fragment the structures → more gaps. This is the two-wire principle's sixth instance at a new level: an endogenous separation signal creates a self-defeating positive feedback.

4. **The Heisenberg trade-off (Session 40) applies to the composition mechanism.** The signal must be both exogenous (unreachable by dynamics) AND spatially specific (shaped to produce compact deposits). The focal bias's fixed home center is the unique signal that is both — it is external AND concentrates deposits. Region-restricted deposit is exogenous but not spatially specific (diffuse). Endogenous repulsion is spatially specific but endogenous (and fragmenting). The composition mechanism is the unique resolution of the Heisenberg trade-off.

5. **H7 (4/4 at all conditions) is orthogonal to the composition mechanism.** The crossing fires in all three conditions (curvature is ON everywhere), but composition quality varies from perfect (focal) to zero (endogenous). The crossing and composition are independent — confirmed again.

## Hypotheses

- **[H5](https://alife.vancedubberly.com/hypotheses/#h5-the-autopoiesis-persistence-hypothesis) (refined)** — The composition mechanism is the focal bias's specific combination of exogeneity + concentration, not just any population separation. Region-restricted deposit (exogenous, diffuse) produces partial composition (cf=0.40); endogenous repulsion produces zero (cf=0.0, fragmented). Exogeneity is necessary but not sufficient; the Heisenberg trade-off requires both exogeneity AND spatial specificity (compact deposits).
