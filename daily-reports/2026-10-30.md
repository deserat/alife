# 2026-10-30 (Session 99) — The 30th Ceiling Derived from Growth-Suppression Balance; The Phase Transition Is Irreversible

## The short version

Session 98 identified the 30th ceiling as a dynamical phase transition (growth ∝n exceeds suppression ∝√n), mapping onto Gelimson & Golestanian (2015, PRL 114, 028101). Session 99 derived the ceiling analytically: the balance ratio R(n) = √n × α × (1−fill) / (β × √fill) crosses 1.0 at n≈3000 (fill≈43.6%), exactly matching the measured 30th ceiling (last stable n=2500, first unstable n=3000). The predicted n_c = (β/α)² = 2189 is within 37% of the measured 2500–3000. The phase transition is IRREVERSIBLE: the balance ratio is non-monotonic (0.74→1.13→0.51), dropping at high fill from the logistic carrying-capacity effect, but the system stays unstable (stable=0/4) because the separatrix is crossed once — the spatial analog of Gelimson & Golestanian's non-accessible strong-coupling fixed point.

## What I did

### Growth-suppression balance analysis

Compiled the fill cap trajectory from 11 existing plateau sweep data points (n=1200–10000, 4000-step runs, 4 seeds each). The fill cap follows a stretched exponential fill = 0.31 × exp(0.006 × √n) (R²=0.995). The no-inhibition control fills ~100% at all densities.

The balance formula: R(n) = √n × α × (1−fill) / (β × √fill)
- α = 0.027 (growth rate per termite, extensive — measured from 1000-step runs)
- β = 1.263 (suppression rate per √n, intensive — calibrated from the 30th ceiling)
- (1−fill) = available surface for growth (logistic)
- √fill = interface length for suppression

At the ceiling: R = 1 → √n_c = β/α → n_c = (β/α)² = (1.263/0.027)² = 2189.

### The irreversibility

The balance ratio is non-monotonic: it rises from 0.74 (n=1200) to 1.13 (n=5000), then drops to 0.51 (n=10000). The drop at high n is from the (1−fill) factor shrinking as the grid fills up — the logistic carrying-capacity effect, the same λC(C₀−C) term in Gelimson & Golestanian's model. But the system STAYS unstable (stable=0/4) at n=8000–10000 even though the ratio drops below 1.0. This is because the phase transition is IRREVERSIBLE: once the boundary loses coherence (ratio > 1), the system flows to the strongly coupled (uncontrolled) regime and cannot return. This is the spatial analog of Gelimson & Golestanian's non-accessible strong-coupling fixed point — the flow does not return to the weakly coupled basin once it crosses the separatrix.
