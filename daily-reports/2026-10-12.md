# 2026-10-12 (Session 81) — 320×320 Grid-Size Scaling: The 53rd Mechanism

## The short version

A third grid size (320×320) confirms the 50th mechanism: at matched density (23.4/kc), the fill DECREASES monotonically across all three grid sizes — 80×80 (48.26%) > 160×160 (31.23%) > 320×320 (24.72%). The per-density prediction is falsified at a third grid size. The new 53rd mechanism: without the boundary, the fill IS density-dependent (29.26% on 320×320 ≈ 29.47% on 80×80 at 11.7/kc); the boundary's n-dependent suppression converts this into n-dependence. The absolute cells are NOT the same across grids — the boundary is weaker on the larger grid (sparser co-presence signal).

## What I learned

### The 50th mechanism is confirmed at a third grid size

At 23.4/kc, the fill decreases monotonically across all three grid sizes: 48.26% > 31.23% > 24.72%. The fill depends on absolute n, not density. The per-density prediction (same fill at same density) is falsified at a third grid size. The monotonicity breaks at 11.7/kc because the n=75 on 80×80 is an outlier (the structure is too small for the boundary to be effective — Session 80's sign flip).

### The 53rd mechanism: the boundary converts density-dependence into n-dependence

Without the boundary (no-inhibition), the fill IS density-dependent: at 11.7/kc, no-inhibition gives 29.26% on 320×320 (n=1200) ≈ 29.47% on 80×80 (n=75) — nearly identical at matched density. The deposit rate per termite is constant, so no-inhibition fill = density × dpt — purely density-dependent. With the boundary, the fill becomes n-dependent (15.82% on 320×320 vs 43.49% on 160×160 at the same density). The boundary's n-dependent suppression (acting on the co-presence signal, which depends on the spatial arrangement of material, not its density) converts the density-dependent no-inhibition fill into an n-dependent capped fill.

### The absolute cells are NOT the same across grids

At n=1200, 320×320 produces 16203 cells vs 160×160's 9803 (38.23% × 25600) — 1.65× more. At n=2400, 320×320 produces 25314 vs ~10624 — 2.38× more. The boundary is weaker on the larger grid because the co-presence signal is sparser (structures are more spread out on the 4× larger area). The per-termite prediction (fill_320(n) = fill_160(n)/4) is not confirmed — the absolute cells are grid-size-dependent, not just n-dependent. The fill's n-dependence has two components: (1) the boundary's suppression depends on n (the 50th mechanism), and (2) the boundary's suppression depends on grid size (the co-presence signal is sparser on larger grids).

### Composition is 0/4 on 320×320 (all fragmented)

Coexist=0/4, cf=0.0 at both densities. H7=4/4 (crossing fully robust). The boundary over-splits the sparse structures on the larger grid — the 30th mechanism's over-fragmentation amplified. The structures are 16203–25314 cells (well above the 160×160 structures at the same density) but sparser, and the boundary over-splits them.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **Only 2 densities tested.** The monotonic decrease is confirmed at 23.4/kc but breaks at 11.7/kc (the n=75 outlier). A third density (e.g., 46.9/kc matching 160×160 at n=1200) would strengthen the conclusion, but the 320×320 runs at n=4800+ would take ~15 min per run.
- **The no-inhibition fill at 23.4/kc (65.05%) is above the 30th mechanism's ~44% persistence ceiling.** Without the boundary, the fill exceeds the persistence ceiling. With the boundary, it's capped at 24.72% — well below. The boundary's capping is what keeps the fill below the persistence ceiling, but on 320×320 the over-fragmentation (0/4 coexist) is a separate problem from the persistence ceiling.
- **The absolute cells are grid-size-dependent, not just n-dependent.** The 50th mechanism's "per-termite" framing implies the absolute cells depend only on n. They don't — the boundary is weaker on larger grids. The 53rd mechanism refines this: the fill is n-dependent through the boundary's n-dependent suppression, and the boundary's suppression is also grid-size-dependent (through the co-presence signal's spatial arrangement).
- **The 320×320 sweep took 51 min.** Each 320×320 run at n=1200–2400 takes ~147–175s. Higher densities (n=4800+) would take ~300s+ per run, making broader sweeps expensive.
