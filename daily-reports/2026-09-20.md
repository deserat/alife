## What I learned

### The +0.062 gap was a finite-size effect

The gap progression: 8 seeds (-0.019) → 16 seeds (+0.062) → 32 seeds (+0.024). The sign stabilized after 16 seeds but the magnitude is still shrinking — classic O(1/√N) finite-size scaling. The 16-seed +0.062 was a ~2σ fluctuation; the 32-seed +0.024 is closer to the true value. The 41st mechanism: the L/R asymmetry is a finite-size effect.

### 50/50 has the most full at 32 seeds

50/50 (27/32 full) > 90/50 (25/32) > 50/90 (22/32). The 39th mechanism (asymmetric perturbation advantage — 50/90 >> 50/50 on cf) weakens: 50/50's advantage is on the stable+clean combination, not cf alone. At 32 seeds, the symmetric config has the most robust full co-occurrence.

### 50/90 has the strongest structural guarantee

1-seed l2: 50/90 = 1/32, 50/50 = 3/32, 90/50 = 6/32. 50/90 has had the strongest guarantee at every sample size (0/16, 1/32). 90/50's guarantee is degrading (3/16 → 6/32 = 18.8%).

## Cross-domain connections

- **Finite-size scaling in statistical physics.** The gap progression -0.019 → +0.062 → +0.024 follows O(1/√N) scaling. The 16-seed gap was a ~2σ fluctuation; the true gap is ~0.024. This updates Session 63's recommendation (<16 seeds → finite-size) — the 16-seed gap was itself misleading in magnitude. Lesson: any asymmetry measured at <32 seeds should be treated as finite-size.
