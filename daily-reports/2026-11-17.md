# 2026-11-17 (Session 114) — The Boundary Is a Marginal Stabilizer, Not a Composition Mechanism

## The short version

The boundary role test (64 runs, 4 fill levels, matched fill with/without boundary) found the boundary is a **marginal stabilizer at the fill-driven phase transition (~42% fill)**, not a composition mechanism. At matched fill: below the ceiling (~35%), composition is perfect without the boundary (cf=1.0, stable=4/4); at the ceiling (~42%), the boundary marginally helps (cf +0.10, stable +1/4); above the ceiling (~48%), both fragment. The fill (deposit/decay equilibrium) determines composition quality, not the boundary. The Gompertz-to-logistic concept is revised (#282).

## What I tested

The boundary role test compares composition quality (coexist_frac, stable_l2, l2_outcome) with and without the boundary at MATCHED FILL — using material_decay (not n) to control the fill, so both conditions sit at the same deposit/decay equilibrium.

4 (n, decay) pairs × 4 seeds × {2-seed, 1-seed} × {no-inhibition, with-boundary} = 64 runs on 160×160.

Pairs:
1. n=2500, decay=0.002 → ~42% fill (the 30th ceiling)
2. n=2500, decay=0.004 → ~35% fill (below the ceiling — stable regime)
3. n=5000, decay=0.002 → ~48% fill (above the ceiling — unstable regime)
4. n=3500, decay=0.002 → ~44% fill (right at the ceiling)

## What this means

1. **The boundary does NOT cap the fill** (fill gap < 1pp at all pairs) — confirmed Session 112.

2. **Below the ceiling (~35%): the boundary does NOTHING.** Both conditions: cf=1.000, stable=4/4, coexist=4/4. The curvature channel + ID tagging + focal bias alone produce perfect composition. The boundary is unnecessary at low fill.

3. **At the ceiling (~42%): the boundary marginally helps.** CF +0.10, stable +1/4. This is the ONLY fill level where the boundary improves composition quality. The boundary slightly reduces fragmentation at the phase boundary.

4. **Above the ceiling (~44–48%): the boundary does NOT help.** Both conditions fragment (0/4 stable, 0/4 coexist at ~48%). The boundary cannot prevent the phase transition. At ~44%, the boundary slightly WORSENS rc (6.5→8.5) — it over-splits the right region.

5. **1-seed: identical everywhere.** The boundary has zero effect on 1-seed (B=0 for 1-seed — co-presence is structurally zero when only one ID exists).

6. **The 64th mechanism: the boundary is a marginal stabilizer at the fill-driven phase transition, not a composition mechanism.** The composition quality is determined by the fill (deposit/decay equilibrium), not by the boundary. The boundary slightly reduces fragmentation AT the transition (~42%) but cannot shift the transition. Below the ceiling, composition is perfect without it; above the ceiling, composition fails with it.

7. **The Gompertz-to-logistic concept is revised (#282).** The three thresholds (30th ~44%, 44th ~75%, 43rd ~100%) are fill fractions at which composition stability breaks down — properties of the deposit/decay equilibrium, not of the boundary's coherent suppression. The design principle is revised: tune material_decay to keep the deposit/decay equilibrium below ~44% fill, not tune the boundary gain.

8. **Cross-domain: phase transitions ↔ marginal stabilizers.** In statistical mechanics, an external field near a phase transition can modulate but not shift it — the transition is driven by temperature (the primary variable). Our boundary is the external field; the fill is the temperature. The 30th ceiling (~44%) is the critical "temperature" for stable composition.
