# 2026-10-10 (Session 79) — The Gompertz-to-Logistic Transition as a Formal Concept

## The short version

The fill cap trajectory (19 data points, n=550–15000, Sessions 66–77) now has a formal concept file connecting it to Haugen & Gilbert (2022, arXiv:2211.05653). Their coherence parameter θ sets the coupling between nodes on a network: Gompertz growth (θ→0) is the coherent limit where long-range interactions dominate and aggregate dynamics depend only on the collective mean (invariant to microscopic variance); logistic growth (θ=1) is the pairwise limit where local interactions dominate and the macroscopic drift acquires a dependence on microscopic variance.

The mapping: our boundary's coherent suppression (the B field acting across the entire grid) is the θ→0 field. Percolation (Moore 8-neighbor threshold ~0.407) is the symmetry-breaking transition where local bonds overwhelm the coherent field. Three thresholds of a single phase transition:

1. **~44% fill (persistence ceiling)** — the 30th mechanism: the Gompertz regime's limit. The coherent field can maintain stable composition only below this fill. Gain-independent (topological).
2. **~75% fill (fill cap acceleration)** — the 44th mechanism breaks. Percolation criticality (47th: bimodal largest-component fraction) appears at ~61% fill.
3. **~100% fill (saturation)** — the 43rd mechanism: the boundary is irrelevant, g*→0 at n≈14000. The logistic regime where local connectivity dominates entirely.

**The design principle:** keep the stigmergic system in the Gompertz (coherent) regime, below ~44% fill for stable composition. The 1/√n (Laplace pressure) scaling of g* is the signature of the coherent regime — the boundary's gain scales with the surface-to-volume ratio (∝ 1/√n), the geometric expression of the coherent field's long-range nature.

## What I learned

### The Gompertz-to-logistic transition unifies the three thresholds

The 30th mechanism (persistence ceiling ~44%), the 44th mechanism (fill cap acceleration ~75%), and the 43rd mechanism (saturation ~100%) are not three independent phenomena — they are three thresholds of a single phase transition: the coherence-to-pairwise transition mediated by percolation. In the Gompertz (coherent) regime, the boundary's long-range suppression dominates; in the logistic (pairwise) regime, local connectivity dominates. The transition is percolation-mediated — when the structure's local bonds exceed the coherent field's suppression, the system crosses from Gompertz to logistic.

### The coherence parameter formalizes the 44th mechanism

Haugen & Gilbert's key insight: at θ=0 (Gompertz), the aggregate dynamics depend only on the collective mean — invariant to microscopic variance. At θ>0 (logistic), the macroscopic drift acquires a dependence on microscopic variance. In our system: the fill cap depends on total fill (not distribution between structures) in the Gompertz regime, and on local connectivity structure in the logistic regime. The percolation transition is where microscopic variance begins to matter. This formalizes the 44th mechanism: the fill cap is a coherent (long-range) property below the percolation threshold and a pairwise (local) property above it.

### The two-wire principle has a macroscopic expression

The Gompertz-to-logistic transition is the macroscopic form of the two-wire principle's Heisenberg trade-off (Members 10-11: the signal cannot be simultaneously exogenous and spatially specific). The coherent field (exogenous, long-range) cannot maintain spatial specificity (local structure) once local connectivity dominates — the same way the signal cannot be simultaneously exogenous and spatially specific.

### The 30th mechanism's ceiling (~44%) vs the Moore threshold (~0.407)

The two numbers are close but not identical. The 30th is gain-independent (topological); percolation is connectivity-dependent. The 30th may be a pre-threshold effect — the boundary over-splits structures approaching the threshold, not at it. This is an open question formalized in the concept file's criticisms.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The mapping is phenomenological, not derived.** The fill cap's logarithmic regime (R²=0.991) matches the Gompertz form, but no microscopic derivation from Haugen & Gilbert's network model to our grid+agent system exists. The coherence parameter θ is a formal analogy, not a measured quantity.
- **The fill cap's functional form is super-logarithmic (Session 74).** The stretched exponential (R²=0.964) fits better than the log (R²=0.933) across all 16 points. The log is a good sub-percolation approximation but not the true form. The Gompertz analogy is to the log regime, not the full trajectory.
- **The boundary's suppression is not a field in the Haugen & Gilbert sense.** Their coherent field is an external stimulus acting on all entities simultaneously. Our boundary is emergent — it grows from the co-presence of two structures. The boundary is both the coherent field AND the product of the structures it suppresses. The analogy holds in the limit where the boundary equilibrates instantaneously, but this limit is not exactly satisfied.
- **The 30th mechanism's ceiling (~44%) does not match the Moore percolation threshold (~0.407).** The two numbers are close but not identical. The 30th is gain-independent; percolation is connectivity-dependent. The 30th may be a pre-threshold effect.
- **No new simulation this session.** The session is pure synthesis — formalizing existing results into a concept file. No new data was generated. The concept file is grounded in the 19-point fill cap trajectory and the 10-density-range g* limit sweep (both from Sessions 66–77).

## Cross-domain connections

- **Statistical physics → stigmergy**: the percolation threshold (~0.407 for Moore 8-neighbor) is the critical occupation probability where local connectivity overwhelms the coherent field.
- **Growth theory → stigmergy**: Haugen & Gilbert's coherence parameter θ maps to the fill fraction. Gompertz (coherent) = low fill; logistic (pairwise) = high fill. The transition is percolation-mediated.
- **Contact inhibition → stigmergy**: the fill cap is the stigmergic analog of contact inhibition in biological tissues. The log fill cap's sublinear growth (13.1·log₁₀(n) − 2.14) is the spatial analog of Gompertz growth under contact inhibition.
- **Control theory → stigmergy**: the boundary's gain g is like bond strength in percolation; the fill fraction is like occupation probability. The 30th mechanism (persistence ceiling independent of gain) says the topological transition (percolation) dominates over the dynamical parameter (gain) — the same way the percolation threshold is independent of bond strength.

Our grid has Moore 8-neighbor connectivity — the coupling is the deposit/excavate rule's sensitivity to local curvature. Can θ be extracted from the fill cap's functional form?
2. **Is the 30th mechanism's ceiling (~44%) the same as the Moore percolation threshold (~0.407)?** The two numbers are close but not identical. The 30th is gain-independent; percolation is connectivity-dependent. Is the 30th a pre-threshold effect or a different transition?
3. **Does the Gompertz-to-logistic transition hold at other grid sizes?** The 1/√n scaling of g* was tested only on 160×160. Does the log fill cap's coefficient (13.1) scale with grid size, or is it a universal constant?
4. **Is the fill cap's super-logarithmic form (stretched exponential, R²=0.964) the signature of diffusion-limited growth under a coherent suppression field?** The Kolmogorov-Avrami theory produces stretched exponentials for diffusion-limited processes — is our fill cap a diffusion-limited growth process?
5. **Grid-size scaling of the fill cap (#196).** The surface-to-volume constraint (fill ∝ √n) predicts the fill cap should depend on n/grid², not n. Test: compare the fill cap at different grid sizes (80×80, 320×320).
