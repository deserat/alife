# 2026-11-16 (Session 113) — The Fill Cap is Material Decay

## The short version

The fill cap is overwhelmingly from **material decay** (the deposit/decay equilibrium), not the boundary (falsified Session 112) or the curvature channel's deposit/excavate balance. A material decay sweep (7 values × 2 densities × 4 seeds, no boundary) found a 2× change in decay shifts fill by 42–55pp. The "stretched exponential" fill cap from Session 93 was the deposit/decay equilibrium at fixed decay=0.002 — the √n is the surface-to-volume ratio (effective deposit rate grows sublinearly as √n), not a diffusion-length signature. The DLA-inverse interpretation is retracted. The Gompertz-to-logistic transition concept needs full revision: the three thresholds are decay-dependent equilibria, not universal coherence-to-pairwise phase transitions.

## What this means

1. **The fill cap is from material decay.** The fill is the equilibrium between deposit rate (∝ n × deposit_prob) and decay rate (∝ decay × fill × grid²). At equilibrium: fill ∝ n × deposit_prob / (decay × grid²). Doubling n or halving decay both increase the equilibrium fill.

2. **The "stretched exponential" (Session 93) was the deposit/decay equilibrium at fixed decay=0.002.** The √n in fill ∝ exp(b·√n) is the surface-to-volume ratio: as the structure grows (area ∝ n), its surface grows as √n, and the effective deposit rate (limited to surface cells by the curvature channel's deposit-at-concavities routing) grows as √n. The stretched exponential is a deposit/decay equilibrium with sublinearly growing deposit rate — not a diffusion-limited aggregation signature.

3. **The DLA-inverse interpretation (Session 93) is retracted.** The √n was not the diffusion length of the boundary's suppression range. It is the surface-to-volume ratio of the growing structure. The "diffusion-limited signature" was a spurious connection to DLA theory.

4. **The Gompertz-to-logistic transition concept needs full revision.** The three thresholds (30th ~44%, 44th ~75%, 43rd ~100%) are decay-dependent equilibria, not universal coherence-to-pairwise phase transitions. Changing material_decay shifts all three. The Haugen & Gilbert coherence parameter θ may map to the decay rate (which sets the growth-removal equilibrium), not the boundary gain.

5. **The separation of concerns in stigmergic systems:** fill (volume) = deposit/decay equilibrium; morphology (shape) = curvature channel (deposit at concavities, excavate at convexities); composition (coexistence) = boundary (preventing merging). Three separate mechanisms on three separate axes.

6. **H7 is robust to decay changes** above ~20% fill. The crossing fires at all decays except 0.032 (too little material for the curvature structure to form).
