# 2026-10-27 (Session 96) — The 30th Ceiling Is the Structure-to-Boundary Ratio

## The short version

The 30th stability ceiling — the fill fraction above which stable composition becomes impossible at any gain — scales with the ratio of structure size to boundary width (dilation radius / grid size), not with absolute structure size. Varying the dilation radius on 160×160 at fixed n shifts the ceiling: radius=4 (2.5% of grid width) → ~40% fill with over-splitting (lc/rc > 3); radius=8 (5%) → ~43.6% with over-splitting (lc/rc > 5); 80×80 radius=8 (10%) → ~64–76% with under-splitting (lc/rc < 1). The degradation mode shifts from over-splitting (narrow boundary, diffusion-limited) to under-splitting (wide boundary, interface-controlled) — the spatial analog of the KJMA crossover (Session 93). The 30th ceiling is where the boundary's spatial granularity matches the structure's: too fine and it fragments, too coarse and it over-compacts.

## What I did

### Dilation radius sweep on 160×160

Varied the co-presence dilation radius (4, 8, 16) on the 160×160 grid at fixed n (1500–3000). The dilation radius controls the boundary's spatial reach — a wider radius means the boundary's suppression extends further into each structure.

**160×160, radius=4 (2.5% of grid width):**
- n=1500, g=0.001: fill=39.5%, stable=2/4, coexist=4/4, h7=4/4, lc=3.06, rc=3.49
- n=2000, g=0.001: fill=41.3%, stable=0/4, coexist=3/4, h7=4/4, lc=3.81, rc=5.55

**160×160, radius=8 (5% of grid width, baseline):**
- n=1500, g=0.003: fill=39.4%, stable=3/4, coexist=4/4, h7=4/4
- n=2000, g=0.001: fill=41.2%, stable=3/4, coexist=4/4, h7=4/4
- n=2500, g=0.001: fill=42.1%, stable=2/4, coexist=4/4, h7=4/4, lc=4.1, rc=4.5
- n=3000, g=0.001: fill=43.6%, stable=0/4, coexist=3/4, h7=4/4, lc=5.5, rc=6.8

**80×80, radius=8 (10% of grid width, from Session 95):**
- n=300 (fill 64%): stable=4/4, lc≈1, rc≈1 (under-splitting)
- n=500 (fill 73%): stable=2/4
- n=600 (fill 76%): stable=2/4 → 1/4

### Key comparison

At the same fill (~41%), radius=4 gives stable=0/4 while radius=8 gives stable=3/4. The 30th ceiling shifted down ~3pp with a 2× narrower boundary. At fill ~39.5%, radius=4 gives stable=2/4 while radius=8 gives stable=3/4 (at g=0.003). The ceiling is NOT about absolute structure size — it is about the ratio.

### The over-splitting → under-splitting crossover

A narrow boundary (2.5% of grid) produces over-splitting (lc/rc > 3) — the fine-grained suppression pattern fragments the structure. A wide boundary (10% of grid) produces under-splitting (lc/rc < 1) — the coarse-grained suppression over-compacts the structure. The crossover happens between 5% and 10% of the grid width.

This is the spatial analog of the KJMA crossover (Session 93): the narrow boundary is diffusion-limited (stretched exponential, fine-grained, over-splitting); the wide boundary is interface-controlled (compressed exponential, coarse-grained, under-splitting). The 30th ceiling is where the boundary's spatial granularity matches the structure's — the matched point between diffusion-limited fragmentation and interface-controlled compaction.

### r=16 limitation

The r=16 test (10% of grid on 160×160) was not completed — 16 Moore dilation passes per step makes 160×160 runs intractably slow (~75s per run). The 80×80 r=8 data (10% of grid) serves as the equivalent: the 30th ceiling is at 64–76% with under-splitting, confirming the ratio-dependence.

### Determinism

Verified: two identical runs (r=4, n=1500, g=0.001, seed=42) produce identical results (cells=10037, stable=True, cf=0.550).
