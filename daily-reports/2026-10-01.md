## What I learned

### The fill cap is logarithmic (R²=0.991)

The fill cap rises from ~38% at n=1200 to ~44% at n=3000 — a 6% rise for a 2.5× increase in n. The logarithmic fit fill% ≈ 13.1·log₁₀(n) − 2.14 fits with R²=0.991 across all 6 data points. This is the 44th mechanism's functional form: the boundary caps fill at a level that rises logarithmically with agent count. The structure grows sublinearly because the boundary's suppression scales with the structure's surface area (∝ √n), while the volume scales as n — the surface-to-volume ratio produces the log deceleration.

### The 30th mechanism collapses the persistence condition

At n=3000, stable is 0/4 at ALL gains — the persistence condition fails entirely. The gain-density interaction (Session 70) amplifies until no gain can maintain stable composition. The larger structures (~11000 cells, ~44% fill) have too much surface area for the boundary to maintain without over-splitting.

### The 1-seed structural guarantee is fully lost

l2(1s)=4/4 at both n=2500 and n=3000 — the single structure crosses the midline in all seeds. The 12th member (structure-to-grid ratio) has reached its limit: the bigger single structure (~5800–6100 cells per half) overwhelms the midline even with focal bias. This is the density-dependent expression of the Heisenberg trade-off: the structure cannot be simultaneously dense enough for the crossing and small enough for the boundary to confine it.

## Cross-domain connections

- **The logarithmic fill cap and Gompertz growth.** The fill cap rising as 13.1·log₁₀(n) is the stigmergic analog of Gompertz growth (logarithmic density dependence in contact-inhibited tissues). In Gompertz dynamics, growth rate decelerates as the population approaches a carrying capacity — the dN/dt ∝ N·log(K/N) form. The fill cap is the spatial analog: the structure's growth decelerates as it approaches the boundary's carrying capacity (the fill cap). The boundary acts as a self-limiting growth law: the more it grows, the more surface area the boundary has to suppress further growth, producing the logarithmic deceleration. At ~74% of the 2D percolation threshold, the structure is in the dense-droplet regime — the boundary keeps it from percolating, but the droplet is large enough that the persistence condition collapses and the 1-seed guarantee fails.

- **The surface-to-volume constraint.** The logarithmic fill cap emerges from a geometric constraint: the boundary's suppression scales with the structure's surface area (∝ √n for a 2D compact structure), while the volume scales as n. The ratio volume/surface ∝ √n matches the 1/√n scaling of g* (Session 70). The log fill cap is the integral of this surface-to-volume constraint over agent count — the structure grows sublinearly because the boundary's capacity to suppress grows with the surface, not the volume.
