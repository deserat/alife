# 2026-10-26 (Session 95) — The 30th Ceiling Shifts With Grid Size

## The short version

The 30th mechanism's persistence ceiling (~43.6% fill on 160×160) is NOT a universal fill fraction — it shifts with grid size. On 80×80, stability persists at 64–76% fill, 23–35pp above the Moore percolation threshold (~40.7%) and above the 160×160's 30th ceiling (43.6%). The stability degrades gradually (4/4 at 64% → 3/4 at 70% → 2/4 at 73.5% → 1/4 at 76%) with no sharp collapse — unlike the 160×160's sharp drop (2/4 → 0/4 between 42% and 43.6%). The degradation mode differs: over-splitting on 160×160 (late_mean_lc/rc > 3–5, too many components) vs under-splitting on 80×80 (late_mean_lc/rc < 1.0, structures too compact). The 30th is about absolute structure size relative to the boundary's spatial scale (dilation radius 8 cells), not fill fraction. The 2.93pp gap on 160×160 is coincidental — on 80×80, the 30th ceiling is 30+pp above the Moore threshold. The absolute-spatial-scale principle (#234) explains the shift: the boundary's dilation radius (8 cells = 10% of 80×80 grid width vs 5% of 160×160) is relatively wider on smaller grids.

## What I found

### The 30th ceiling is NOT the Moore threshold — it shifts with grid size

**80×80 plateau:**
- n=300 (fill 64%): stable 4/4, coexist 4/4, h7 4/4
- n=400 (fill 70%): stable 3/4, coexist 3–4/4
- n=500 (fill 73.5%): stable 2/4, coexist 3–4/4
- n=600 (fill 76%): stable 1–2/4, coexist 1–3/4

**160×160 plateau (from Session 94):**
- n=2500 (fill 42%): stable 2/4
- n=3000 (fill 43.6%): stable 0/4 (collapse)

On 80×80, stability persists at fill fractions 20–33pp above the 160×160's 30th ceiling. The 30th ceiling is NOT at the same fill fraction on both grids.

### The degradation is gradual, not a sharp collapse

On 160×160, stability drops from 2/4 (42% fill) to 0/4 (43.6% fill) — a sharp 1.5pp collapse. On 80×80, stability degrades from 4/4 (64%) to 1/4 (76%) over a 12pp range — a smooth crossover, not a phase transition.

### The degradation mode differs: over-splitting vs under-splitting

On 160×160 at the 30th ceiling, late_mean_lc and late_mean_rc exceed 3–5 (over-splitting — the boundary creates too many components per region). On 80×80, late_mean_lc and late_mean_rc are below 1.0 (under-splitting — the structures are too compact, the boundary can't create enough space between them). The 80×80's degradation is grid saturation, not over-splitting. The 30th mechanism (over-splitting) doesn't occur on 80×80 because the grid is too small for the structures to reach the absolute size at which the boundary over-splits.

### The boundary's fill-capping power is negligible on 80×80

The fill with and without the boundary are nearly identical:
- n=300: 64.3% (with) vs 65.0% (without)
- n=500: 73.5% (with) vs 73.6% (without)
- n=600: 76.1% (with) vs 76.5% (without)

The curvature channel alone (deposit/excavate balance) caps the fill at ~76% on 80×80. The boundary adds stability (1/4 → 2/4 at n=500; 0/4 → 1/4 at n=600) without changing the fill. On 160×160, the boundary IS the primary fill-capping mechanism (87–100% without → ~44% with). The boundary's fill-capping role is grid-size-dependent.

### The absolute-spatial-scale principle explains the shift

The boundary's dilation radius (8 cells) is an absolute spatial scale. On 80×80, 8 cells = 10% of the grid width; on 160×160, 8 cells = 5%. The boundary is relatively wider on 80×80, so it can maintain stable composition at higher fill fractions. The focal bias (1-cell step) is also relatively stronger on 80×80 (1.25% vs 0.625%). The 30th ceiling is about the ratio of the structure's absolute size to the boundary's absolute spatial scale — on 80×80, this ratio is more favorable at any given fill fraction.

- **Percolation theory → stigmergic stability (refined):** The Moore threshold (~0.407) is a universal fill fraction for random site connectivity. The 30th ceiling is NOT universal — it's a boundary-mechanism threshold that depends on the ratio of the boundary's absolute spatial scales to the grid size. The proximity to the Moore threshold on 160×160 is coincidental (the boundary's dilation radius happens to produce over-splitting near the Moore fill on that grid size). On 80×80, the same boundary mechanism produces over-splitting (if it can occur at all) at a much higher fill fraction.
- **Absolute-spatial-scale principle (#234) → 30th ceiling:** The principle says any mechanism acting through an absolute spatial scale converts density-dependence into n-dependence. The 30th ceiling is an instance: the boundary's dilation radius (8 cells) is an absolute spatial scale whose effectiveness depends on the grid size. On smaller grids, the boundary is relatively wider, so the 30th ceiling is at a higher fill fraction.
