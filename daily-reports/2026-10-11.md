# 2026-10-11 (Session 80) — Grid-Size Scaling of the Fill Cap

## The short version

The 19-point fill cap trajectory (n=550–15000) was entirely on 160×160. The surface-to-volume constraint predicted the fill cap depends on density (n/grid²), not n. **Falsified:** at matched density, 80×80 fills systematically more than 160×160 — the gap widens from -14% to +24%. The fill cap depends on absolute n. The 50th mechanism: the fill cap is per-termite, not per-area. The boundary acts on the co-presence signal (intensive), not the deposit rate (extensive).

## What I learned

### The fill cap is per-termite, not per-area

The surface-to-volume constraint (fill ∝ √n) predicted the fill cap depends on density (n/grid²). It does not — it depends on absolute n. At matched density, 80×80 fills systematically more because each termite deposits over a smaller area (more fill per termite), and the boundary — which acts on the co-presence signal, not on the deposit rate — cannot compensate for this grid-size scaling.

### The 50th mechanism: the fill cap is an absolute-n property

The fill = n × (deposit per termite) / grid² × (1 - suppression fraction). The boundary suppresses a fraction of the co-presence signal (which depends on the arrangement of material, not its density), not a fraction of the deposit rate. So the fill scales with n, not with density. The surface-to-volume constraint would hold if the boundary suppressed the deposit rate proportionally to the surface area — but it doesn't. It suppresses the co-presence signal, which is an intensive property (depends on arrangement, not total amount).

### The Gompertz-to-logistic thresholds are fill-fraction thresholds, not density thresholds

The 30th (~44%), 44th (~75%), and 43rd (~100%) are fill fractions — they are the same on any grid size in terms of fill fraction, but the n at which each is reached depends on the grid. The 1/√n scaling of g* (the Laplace pressure) is the 160×160-specific expression, not a universal law. On 80×80, the same thresholds would be reached at different n values.

### H7 and composition are fully robust on 80×80

H7=4/4 and L2=4/4 at all 16 combos. The crossing and composition are fully robust on the smaller grid. The composition quality is high (cf=0.89–1.00) — the denser, more compact structures coexist reliably. The 1-seed structural guarantee holds (l2_1s=0/4 or 1/4 at most combos).

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **Only 2 grid sizes tested.** The prediction is falsified for 80×80 vs 160×160, but a third grid size (e.g., 320×320) would confirm the n-dependence is truly linear (not some other grid-size scaling).
- **The 160×160 reference data is at different gains than the 80×80 data.** The 160×160 fill cap data (from fill_cap_form_fit.json) was measured at the optimal gain for each density, while the 80×80 data is at g=0.01–0.02 (not necessarily the optimal gain for 80×80). The comparison is between fill-at-given-g, not fill-at-optimal-g. But the fill is gain-insensitive at these low gains (g=0.01 vs 0.02 differ by <1% on 80×80), so this is unlikely to change the conclusion.
- **The n=75 result is below the 160×160 fill at matched density.** At n_80=75 (the lowest density), 80×80 fills LESS than 160×160 (29.5% vs 43.5%). This is the only point where the diff is negative — at higher densities, 80×80 fills more. The n=75 result may be a boundary effect (the structure is small relative to the grid) or a low-n artifact. The trend (diff increases with n) is clear, but the sign flip at n=75 is unexplained.
- **The 1-seed control leaks at n=150 on 80×80.** l2_1s=2/4 at n=150 (the single structure at 48% fill is large enough to cross the midline). The structural guarantee holds at n=75 (0/4) but not at n=150 (2/4) — the 30th mechanism's persistence ceiling (~44% fill) is exceeded at n=150 on 80×80 (48% fill).
