# 2026-11-26 (Session 121) — The Ceiling Is Not the Moore Threshold: Structures Percolate at 21% Fill

## The short version

The ~42% persistence ceiling is ~1.3pp above the Moore 8-neighbor site percolation threshold (~40.407). Queued-topic #301 asked: is this causal or coincidental?

**It is coincidental.** The structures percolate at 21% fill — each individual ID's structure has one giant component (largest_frac ~0.995) spanning the grid (4/4 seeds), while random-site percolation at 21% fill spans only 16/100 realizations (mean 1793 components, largest_frac=0.007). The curvature channel + focal bias produce spatially correlated deposits that are far more connected than random at the same fill fraction. The ceiling is a geometric over-splitting limit (the focal bias cannot maintain two compact populations above ~42% fill), not a connectivity threshold. The ~1.3pp gap is coincidental — the ceiling is about compactness, the Moore threshold is about connectivity, on different axes.

## The 71st mechanism

The ~42% persistence ceiling is NOT the Moore percolation threshold (~40.7%). The structures percolate at 21% fill (4/4 per-ID spanning) where random-site percolation does not (16/100). The ceiling is a geometric over-splitting limit (lc/rc > 5) — the focal bias cannot maintain two compact populations above ~42% fill, regardless of connectivity. The ~1.3pp gap between the ceiling and the Moore threshold is coincidental: the ceiling is about compactness (spatial arrangement breaking down), the Moore threshold is about connectivity (random-site percolation). These are on different axes with different fill dependencies.
