# 2026-10-31 (Session 100) — Growth-Phase MSD Matches G&G 2D α=1.72, But Is Not the Steady-State Critical Exponent

## The short version

The cell count MSD exponent α_nc = 1.729 at n=1500 (R²=0.998) matches Gelimson & Golestanian's (2015, PRL 114, 028101) 2D critical exponent α = 2/z = 1.72 to 3 significant figures. But this is the GROWTH-PHASE exponent, not the steady-state critical exponent. At 2000 steps, the fill values (48–75%) are all above the steady-state 30th ceiling (~43.6%) — the system is in the growth/overshoot phase. The exponent decreases monotonically with n (1.73 → 1.64 → 1.47 → 1.26) as growth saturates. The component count exponents (α_lc, α_rc) are subdiffusive (~0.5–0.8). Methodology lesson: the growth-phase MSD exponent is NOT the steady-state critical exponent — the detrended MSD removes the mean drift but not the variance growth, so during growth the MSD is dominated by growth dynamics, not steady-state fluctuations.

## What I did

### MSD measurement

Used `run_two_region_hetero` (the same function as all plateau sweeps) with 2000 steps, sample_every=10 (200 samples), dual mode g_form=g_persist=0.001, focal bias=0.3, jitter=10, at 4 densities (n=1500, 2500, 3000, 4000). The detrended MSD:

MSD(τ) = Var(C(t+τ) − C(t)) = <(ΔC)²> − <ΔC>²

where C(t) is the total cell count (n_structure_cells) at sample t. The detrending removes the mean growth drift. The power-law fit: log(MSD) = α · log(τ) + c, for τ ≥ 3.

### Results

| n | fill% | α_nc | R² | α_lc | α_rc | mean comps |
|---|-------|------|-----|------|------|------------|
| 1500 | 48.1 | **1.729** | 0.998 | 0.701 | 0.833 | 5.2 |
| 2500 | 59.8 | 1.643 | 0.995 | 0.832 | 0.706 | 4.3 |
| 3000 | 65.8 | 1.469 | 0.988 | 0.738 | 0.657 | 4.0 |
| 4000 | 74.9 | 1.255 | 0.982 | 0.515 | 0.367 | 3.7 |

**α_nc = 1.729 at n=1500 matches G&G's 2D α = 1.72 to 3 sig figs.** G&G's α = 2/z = 2/1.16 = 1.72 in 2D.

### The growth-phase problem

The fill values (48–75%) are all above the steady-state 30th ceiling (~43.6%). At 2000 steps, the system is in the growth/overshoot phase — the structure has filled past the ceiling and is relaxing back. The α_nc exponent measures the growth dynamics, not the steady-state fluctuations. The exponent decreases monotonically with n because the growth saturates at higher fill.

### The component count exponents

The per-region component count exponents (α_lc, α_rc) are subdiffusive (~0.5–0.8) — the boundary fragments are more stable than the total cell count. The total cell count includes both regions and reflects the global growth dynamics; the per-region counts reflect the boundary's local fragmentation dynamics, which are subdiffusive (the boundary holds the fragments in place).

### Methodology lesson (#260)

The growth-phase MSD exponent is NOT the steady-state critical exponent. The detrended MSD removes the mean drift but not the variance growth — during growth, Var(C) itself grows with time, so the MSD reflects the growth dynamics, not the steady-state fluctuations. The critical exponent must be measured at steady state (4000+ steps), from the late-window fluctuations only. This is the same lesson as the irreversibility: a measurement during the transient does not characterize the steady state.

## Cross-domain connections

- **G&G 2D critical exponent (α=1.72) ↔ stigmergic growth dynamics (α_nc=1.73 at n=1500).** The cell count growth dynamics at the lowest density have the same power-law exponent as G&G's dynamical RG critical point in 2D. The match is to 3 sig figs (1.729 vs 1.72). But the G&G exponent is a steady-state critical property, while ours is a growth-phase property — the connection may be genuine (the growth dynamics transiently pass through the critical regime) or coincidental (the growth law produces α ≈ 1.7 for unrelated reasons).
