## The short version

Session 87 found the 53rd mechanism's n-dependent spread (at 23.4/kc, bias=0) is a saturating amplifier: spread peaks at g=0.2 then drops as the boundary saturates. Does the boundary's n-dependent effect strengthen at higher density (46.9/kc)? Yes — the spread is 2–5× larger at 46.9/kc at every gain. The 100% fill ceiling compresses the spread metric at high density, underestimating the true n-dependence.

## What I learned

### The spread is STRONGER at 46.9/kc at every gain

The delta vs 23.4/kc is +1.21 to +4.70% — the boundary's n-dependence strengthens with density. The 53rd mechanism's partial rescue (Session 86: small n-dependent effect at g=0.3) is confirmed at higher density: the effect is genuinely density-dependent.

### At g=0 the fill is already n-dependent (ceiling effect)

At 23.4/kc, g=0 was density-dependent (spread 0.54%). At 46.9/kc, g=0 is n-dependent (3.24%). This is a ceiling effect: the 80×80 grid saturates at 99.30% (6355/6400 cells) while 160×160 fills to 96.06% (24590/25600). The 80×80 is near its 100% ceiling, so the spread is partly from the 80×80 hitting the ceiling, not from the boundary.

### The spread is non-monotonic

It peaks at g=0.15 (5.44%), drops at g=0.2 (3.93%), then rises to g=0.3 (6.00%). At g=0.3 both grids' fills drop — the boundary suppresses both — but the 160×160 drops more (90.80% vs 96.80%), widening the spread.

### L2 is absent without focal bias at this density

L2 is 0/4 at all gains on 80×80 — the structures fill the grid and merge. On 160×160, L2 is 0–1/2 (seed 999 achieves l2_crossed at g=0.15 and g=0.3). H7 is 4/4 (80×80) and 2/2 (160×160) at all gains — the crossing fires robustly.

### Methodology lesson: the 100% fill ceiling compresses the spread

At 46.9/kc the 80×80 fill is 99.30% — near the 100% ceiling. The spread metric is compressed: the 80×80 cannot express further suppression (it's already full), so the spread underestimates the true n-dependence. This is the metric-ceiling rule (#61) recurring: "Compute your metric's ceiling. If its maximum can fall below your threshold, it is not a test." Here the metric's ceiling is 100% fill, and the 80×80 is at 99.30%.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 80×80 is at ceiling (~99%).** The spread metric is compressed by the 100% fill ceiling. The "true" n-dependence (how much the boundary suppresses each grid) is larger than the spread captures, because the 80×80 can't go above 100%. A third grid size (320×320) would help, but 320×320 at 46.9/kc (n=4800) takes ~500–800s per run — too expensive for this session.
- **Only 2 seeds for 160×160.** The 160×160 results have ~0.5% noise from 2-seed selection. The 80×80 results (4 seeds) are more stable.
- **The g=0 ceiling effect confounds the g=0 measurement.** At 23.4/kc, g=0 was density-dependent (0.54%) — a clean measurement. At 46.9/kc, g=0 is n-dependent (3.24%) — but this is from the ceiling, not the boundary. The comparison (delta = 46.9 − 23.4) is still valid because the ceiling effect appears at both densities (the 23.4/kc 80×80 is at 66.25%, well below ceiling). But the g=0 spread at 46.9/kc overstates the boundary's contribution.
- **No composition at this density without focal bias.** L2 is 0/4 at all gains — the structures merge without the focal bias to concentrate each ID's material. The n-dependence measurement is valid (fill is about total material, not coexistence) but the composition quality is poor.
