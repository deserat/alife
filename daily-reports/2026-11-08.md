# 2026-11-08 (Session 105) — The 30th Ceiling Has Two Regimes; the Formula Was Fit to Censored Data

## The short version

Session 104's two-component formula (ceiling = 185.82×(r/grid) + 3822.55×(1/grid) + 10.34, R²=0.996) predicted ceiling = 24.61% at r=4 on 320×320 (r/grid = 1.25%). A sweep of n=2000–5000 (4 n values × 4 seeds + 1-seed controls + no-inhibition controls, 30 runs, 64 minutes) found 0/4 stable at ALL n values, including fill=22.2% — below the predicted 24.6%. The formula overestimates by at least 2.4pp. The 56th mechanism: the 30th ceiling has two regimes. (1) Capping regime (160×160, 80×80 — boundary gap >10%): the boundary caps fill; the ceiling is the fill where growth overwhelms suppression; the formula applies. (2) No-capping regime (320×320 at all r tested — boundary gap <1%): the boundary fragments without capping; stability is 0/4 at all fill; the formula overestimates because there's no suppression to balance against growth. Methodology lesson: the formula was fit to censored data (320×320 ceilings are upper bounds, not exact), inflating R².

### Part 2 — The 56th mechanism: two regimes

The boundary has **zero fill-capping power** at all n values (gap to no-inhibition: +0.4%, −0.8%, −0.6%, −0.2% — all <1%). This matches Sessions 102–103 (r=8 and r=16 on 320×320 also had gap <1%). On 160×160 r=8, the boundary reduces fill from ~100% to ~44% (gap ~56%). On 320×320 at any r, the boundary adds essentially zero capping.

**Over-splitting is severe** (50–77 components per region at all n values). The boundary fragments the structure into many small pieces without preventing growth — suppression is present (b_max = 49–79) but too spatially diffuse.

The 30th ceiling has two regimes:
1. **Capping regime** (160×160, 80×80 — gap >10%): the boundary caps fill; the ceiling is the fill where growth overwhelms suppression (the dynamical phase transition, Session 98); the formula applies because the suppression has a magnitude.
2. **No-capping regime** (320×320 at all r tested — gap <1%): the boundary fragments without capping; stability is 0/4 at ALL fill; the ceiling is effectively 0%; the formula overestimates because there's no suppression to balance against growth.

The regime boundary is the boundary's capping power, not the fill level. The formula models a growth-suppression balance that only exists in the capping regime.

### Part 3 — Methodology lesson: censored data in regression

The formula was fit to 5 data points, but 3 of them (320×320 r=8: "below 25%", 320×320 r=16: "below 32.7%", 320×320 r=4: "below 22.2%") are upper bounds, not exact measurements. The R²=0.996 was inflated by treating upper bounds as exact. The correct approach: either measure the exact ceiling (find the fill where stability transitions from >0/4 to 0/4) or use censored regression (Tobit). The R²=0.996 measured the fit to censored data, not to the true ceilings.

This is the control-arm methodology rule (#75) recurring at the regression level: a formula that responds to data but cannot distinguish exact ceilings from upper bounds is a description, not a test.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Censored data in regression ↔ the ceiling formula's two regimes.** The 320×320 ceiling data points are upper bounds (stability is 0/4 at all tested fills), not exact measurements. Fitting a regression to upper bounds as if they were exact inflates R² and overestimates predictions. The R²=0.996 was the fit to censored data, not to the true ceilings.
- **Control theory ↔ the no-capping regime.** In the capping regime, the boundary acts as a controller (suppression ∝ √n, the diffusion length). In the no-capping regime, the controller has zero gain — the system is open-loop, and the "ceiling" concept (the setpoint at which growth overwhelms feedback) doesn't apply because there is no feedback to overwhelm.
