**g* does NOT hit zero.** The 43rd mechanism confirmed at a seventh density range. H7=4/4 at all 6 plateau combos.

**The fill cap BREAKS the logarithmic fit — the fill cap is accelerating.** At n=4000, fill rises to ~45.8% (11735 cells); at n=5000, ~48.0% (12348 cells). **At ~48% fill (n=5000), the structures are at ~81% of the 2D percolation threshold (~59%).**

## What I learned

### The fill cap breaks the logarithmic fit — it is accelerating (R² drops from 0.991 to 0.984)

The fill cap rises from ~43.6% (n=3000) to ~45.8% (n=4000) to ~48.0% (n=5000) — a 4.4% rise for a 1.67× increase in n. The 6-point log fit (R²=0.991) predicted ~45.1% and ~46.3% — underestimating by +0.7% and +1.7%. The 8-point refit has R²=0.984, lower than the 6-point. The fill cap is **accelerating**: it rises faster than logarithmic at n≥4000. This suggests the 44th mechanism is not a simple Gompertz law but has a super-logarithmic component.

### The 30th mechanism does NOT recover at very low gain

At n=3000, stable collapsed to 0/4 at all gains (0.001–0.005). Testing g=0.0005 (half the previous minimum) at n=4000–5000 found stable 0/4 at all 6 combos — the persistence condition has collapsed and cannot be recovered by lowering gain alone. The structures (~11500–12400 cells, ~46–48% fill) have too much surface area for the boundary to maintain without over-splitting, regardless of gain.

### Composition collapses at n=5000

Coexist is 0/4 at all 3 gains for n=5000 — the first density where composition fails entirely at every gain tested. At n=4000, coexist recovers slightly (3/4 at g=0.003) but stable remains 0/4. The boundary over-splits the larger structures into fragments (late_mean_lc 5.1–6.8, late_mean_rc 5.3–7.4).

### The fill cap is approaching the percolation threshold

At ~48% fill (n=5000), the structures are at ~81% of the 2D percolation threshold (~59%). The 8-point log fit predicts ~52% at n=10000 and ~56.6% at n=20000 — approaching but not yet reaching the percolation threshold. The acceleration suggests the fill cap may eventually reach ~59% at some n where the droplet percolates and the 44th mechanism breaks down entirely.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 4-seed sample is small.** The 0/4 coexist at n=5000 may be a small-sample effect — at 8 or 16 seeds, some coexist may appear (as seen at other densities where 4-seed 0/4 became 8-seed 1/4).
- **The result is confirmatory for g* ≠ 0.** The genuinely new finding is the fill cap's acceleration — the log fit breaks. But the 8-point fit (R²=0.984) is still a reasonable fit; the acceleration is a second-order effect.
- **The 30th mechanism's persistence could be specific to the dual mode.** A different boundary mechanism (e.g., adaptive gain) might maintain stable composition at higher density. But the 30th mechanism has been consistent across all configurations tested.
- **The composition collapse at n=5000 could be specific to the current boundary parameters.** A density-dependent gain (g*(n) from the 1/√n fit) might rescue composition at n=5000. But g=0.0005 is already very low.

## Cross-domain connections

- **The accelerating fill cap and the approach to percolation.** The fill cap's acceleration toward the 2D percolation threshold (~59%) suggests the 44th mechanism is a density-dependent cap that holds only below the percolation transition. The surface-to-volume constraint (boundary suppression ∝ √n, structure volume ∝ n) predicts the fill cap should eventually reach the percolation threshold — the boundary's capping power is finite. The Gompertz analogy (Session 71) was the first-order approximation; the acceleration is the second-order correction. The fill cap may follow a super-logarithmic law that eventually reaches the percolation threshold, at which point the droplet percolates and composition breaks down entirely (as seen at n=5000: coexist 0/4). This connects to the percolation phase transition in statistical physics: the 44th mechanism is a finite-size effect that holds below the critical density.

- **The surface-to-volume constraint (continued).** The fill cap's n-dependence emerges from a geometric constraint: the boundary's suppression scales with the structure's surface area (∝ √n for a 2D compact structure), while the volume scales as n. The ratio volume/surface ∝ √n matches the 1/√n scaling of g*. The log fill cap was the first integral of this constraint; the acceleration is the second-order correction — as the structure grows, its surface area grows sublinearly relative to its volume, and the boundary's capping power weakens. At the percolation threshold (~59% fill), the structure's volume overwhelms its surface-area-mediated suppression entirely.
