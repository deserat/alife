# 2026-10-22 (Session 87) — Finer Gain Sweep: The 53rd Mechanism's Rescue Is Smooth, Not Thresholded

## The short version

Session 86 found the boundary at g=0.3 (bias=0, no focal) produces a small n-dependent effect (spread 3.31% with 3 grids). At g=0 (bias=0) the spread was 2.23% (density-dependent). The open question: where does the transition from negligible to visible n-dependence happen? Is it a sharp phase transition or a gradual crossover?

**The transition is gradual, not a phase transition.** The spread (|fill_80×80 − fill_160×160|) goes: 0.54% (g=0) → 0.72% (g=0.05) → 2.21% (g=0.1) → 2.21% (g=0.15) → 2.72% (g=0.2) → 1.30% (g=0.3). First crossing of 2% at g=0.1 — a smooth 3× jump from g=0.05, not a sharp threshold. The spread is non-monotonic: it peaks at g=0.2 then drops at g=0.3 because the boundary's response curve saturates at very high gain, reducing the differential between grids. The 53rd mechanism is a saturating amplifier with a narrow bandwidth (g≈0.1–0.2), not a phase transition.

## What I learned

### The transition is gradual, not a phase transition

The spread goes from 0.72% (g=0.05) to 2.21% (g=0.1) — a 3× jump, but not a sharp threshold. Below g=0.05, the boundary's suppression is too weak to create a measurable differential. Above g=0.1, the differential is visible but doesn't grow monotonically.

### The spread is non-monotonic in gain

It peaks at g=0.2 (2.72%) then drops at g=0.3 (1.30%) — the boundary saturates at very high gain, suppressing both grids nearly equally (54.92% vs 53.62%), reducing the differential. The 2-grid spread (1.30%) underestimates the 3-grid n-dependence from Session 86 (3.31%) because the 320×320 grid (which drops to 51.61%) is missing.

### The 53rd mechanism is a saturating amplifier, not a threshold

Cross-domain: the non-monotonic spread maps onto a saturating amplifier in control theory. Below the crossover (g<0.1), the controller is too weak; in the bandwidth (g=0.1–0.2), it differentiates (n-dependent); above it (g=0.3), it saturates and the differential collapses. The bandwidth is narrow (g≈0.1–0.2). The 53rd mechanism is not a phase transition (threshold) but a bandpass (saturating amplifier).

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **Only 2 grid sizes tested.** The 2-grid spread underestimates the 3-grid n-dependence (1.30% vs 3.31% at g=0.3). The 320×320 grid was omitted for time (each run takes ~90s). The transition point (g=0.1) is valid with 2 grids, but the non-monotonic drop at g=0.3 is less dramatic than the 3-grid data.
- **Only 2 seeds for 160×160.** The 160×160 g=0.3 result (53.62%) differs from Session 86's 4-seed result (54.14%) due to seed selection. The 80×80 result (54.92%) matches exactly (same 4 seeds). The 2-seed 160×160 adds ~0.5% noise.
- **The 0.54% spread at g=0 has a reversed sign.** 160×160 fills MORE than 80×80 (66.79% vs 66.25%). This is within 2-seed noise, but the sign reversal is worth noting — without the boundary, the larger grid has a tiny advantage (more absolute n → slightly more fill from the density-dependent curvature channel alone).
