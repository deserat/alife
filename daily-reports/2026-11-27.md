# 2026-11-27 (Session 122) — The Fill Equilibrium: fill ∝ n^0.28 / √decay

## The short version

The fill at steady state follows a power law **fill = 0.24 × n^0.28 × decay^(−0.50)** (R² = 0.884, 25 data points on 160×160). The naive deposit/decay equilibrium predicts fill ∝ n/decay (exponents α=1, β=−1). The actual exponents — α=0.28 (sublinear in n) and β=−0.50 (1/√decay, not 1/decay) — reveal that the **deposit rate is not constant**: it depends on the structure's shape, which changes with both n and decay. A smaller structure (high decay) has higher surface-to-volume ratio → higher curvature → higher deposit probability → higher deposit rate. A self-consistent formula with 1/√A surface scaling fails (R² = −0.27) because the curvature channel produces branched morphology, not a compact disk — the surface scaling is steeper than 1/√A.

## The 72nd mechanism

The fill equilibrium follows **fill ∝ n^0.28 / √decay** (R² = 0.884), not the naive fill ∝ n/decay. The deposit rate is structure-dependent — it increases as the structure shrinks (higher surface-to-volume ratio → higher curvature → higher deposit probability). The power-law exponents encode this feedback:

- **α ≈ 0.28** (sublinear in n): the focal bias's concentration weakens with n — more agents wander farther from home, spreading deposits. This is below the √n surface-to-volume prediction (α = 0.5) — a sub-diffusive regime.
- **β ≈ −0.50** (1/√decay, not 1/decay): the deposit rate scales as √decay. At higher decay, the structure is smaller → higher surface-to-volume → higher deposit rate → partially compensates for the faster decay. This is a feedback loop: fill determines structure size → structure size determines deposit rate → deposit rate determines fill.

The self-consistent formula with 1/√A surface scaling fails (R² = −0.27) because the curvature channel produces branched morphology, not a compact disk. The actual surface scaling is steeper than 1/√A, making the surface term in the theoretical formula too small.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Reaction-diffusion with shape-dependent rate constants → stigmergic fill equilibrium.** In chemical kinetics, when the rate constant depends on the reactant's spatial distribution (catalytic surface area changes with concentration), the equilibrium follows a non-trivial power law, not mass-action. Our fill equilibrium is the stigmergic analog: the "rate constant" (deposit probability) depends on the structure's shape, which depends on the fill (the "concentration").
- **The 1/√A failure → fractal surface scaling.** The self-consistent formula's failure suggests the structure is not compact (D_f < 2 in 2D). The actual surface scaling may follow A^((D_f−2)/2) where D_f is the fractal dimension. Measuring D_f (box-counting) would fix the formula.
