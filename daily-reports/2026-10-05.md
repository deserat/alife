## What I learned

### The 44th mechanism fully breaks — the fill cap acceleration is growing

The fill cap at n=10000 (~75%) is far above the stretched exponential prediction (~65.1%). The acceleration increased from +3.4% (n=5000→6000) to +9.4% (n=6000→8000) to +14.5% (n=8000→10000). The acceleration is growing, not plateauing — the boundary's capping power is finite and has been overwhelmed above the percolation threshold. The boundary still retains ~25% of the grid as inter-structure void (fill ~75%, not 100%), but this residual is not a cap — it is the boundary's footprint on the grid.

### The 47th mechanism resolves — the bimodality was the critical regime's signature

At n=8000, the largest-component fraction was bimodal (6/12 at 93–95%, 6/12 at ~48%). At n=10000, all 8 runs have largest_frac ~97.5% — the bimodality collapses to a single mode. The percolation order parameter converges to 1 above the critical regime. The 48th mechanism: the convergence value is ~97.5%, not 100% — the boundary retains ~25% of the grid as inter-structure void. This is the stigmergic analog of the percolated phase: the structure fills the grid except for the boundary's spatial footprint.

### The Moore 8-neighbor threshold correction re-frames Sessions 73–74

The 2D site percolation threshold for Moore (8-neighbor) connectivity is ~0.407, not ~0.593 (which is for von Neumann 4-neighbor). At n=8000 (fill ~60.7%), the system is well above ~0.407 — the bimodality is the boundary holding the structure at the edge of its coherent regime, not random percolation criticality near the threshold. This means the n=8000 bimodality is a boundary-mediated effect, not a percolation critical phenomenon. The system percolated at much lower n (somewhere between n=1200 at 38% fill and n=8000 at 61% fill), and the n=8000 bimodality is the boundary's last stand — the coherent suppression is failing, and the system flips between "boundary holds" (two halves) and "boundary fails" (giant cluster).

### The Gompertz-to-logistic transition is confirmed

Haugen & Gilbert (2022) show Gompertz growth arises from coherent (long-range) interactions — an external field stimulating a response across all entities simultaneously. The boundary's suppression is a coherent field. Below the percolation threshold, the coherent field produces the Gompertz-like fill cap (logarithmic at low density, accelerating at high density). Above the threshold, local connectivity (short-range bonds) overwhelms the coherent field — the Gompertz-to-logistic transition. At n=10000 (~75% fill), the fill accelerates toward 100% (no-inhibition), confirming the departure from coherence.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 4-seed sample is small for the convergence claim.** All 8 runs at n=10000 have largest_frac ~97.5%, but 4 seeds per gain is a small sample. 16 or 32 seeds would confirm the convergence is unimodal, not a narrow bimodality.
- **The Moore threshold correction changes the interpretation of Sessions 73–74.** The n=8000 bimodality was described as "percolation criticality near the 2D threshold (~59%)" — but the correct threshold for our connectivity is ~0.407, and at ~61% fill the system is well above it. The bimodality is post-threshold boundary resistance, not near-threshold criticality. This is a correction, not a refutation — the bimodality is genuine, but its interpretation changes.
- **The fill cap's residual ~25% void is not explained.** The boundary retains ~25% of the grid as inter-structure void (fill ~75%, not 100%) even when the structure is fully percolated. Is this the boundary's spatial footprint (the B field's growth/decay dynamics leave a residual), or is it the structure's own porosity (the curvature channel doesn't fill every cell)? A no-boundary, no-focal-bias run would distinguish these.
- **The 30th mechanism is permanently collapsed above ~44% fill.** This has been confirmed at 9 density ranges (n=3000–10000). The persistence condition may be unachievable at high density — the boundary cannot maintain stable composition when the structures are too large. This may be a fundamental limit of the two-wire principle at high density, not a parameter-tuning problem.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Gompertz-to-logistic transition (Haugen & Gilbert 2022).** Gompertz growth = coherent (long-range) interactions; logistic = pairwise (local). The boundary's coherent suppression produces Gompertz-like fill cap below the percolation threshold; its breakdown above the threshold is the Gompertz-to-logistic transition. Confirmed at n=10000 (~75% fill): the fill accelerates toward 100% (no-inhibition), the coherent field's capping power is irrelevant.
- **Moore vs von Neumann percolation thresholds.** The 2D site percolation threshold depends on connectivity: ~0.407 for Moore (8-neighbor), ~0.593 for von Neumann (4-neighbor). Our sim14 uses Moore connectivity, so the relevant threshold is ~0.407. At n=8000 (~61% fill), the system is well above ~0.407 — the bimodality is post-threshold boundary resistance, not near-threshold criticality.
- **The 48th mechanism as a stigmergic percolation signature.** The spanning cluster fraction converges to ~97.5%, not 100% — the boundary retains ~25% of the grid as inter-structure void. This is the stigmergic analog of the percolated phase: the structure fills the grid except for the boundary's spatial footprint. In random percolation, the giant component fills 100% above the threshold; in stigmergic percolation, the boundary's coherent suppression leaves a residual void even when the structure percolates.
