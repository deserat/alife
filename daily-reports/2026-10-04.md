# 2026-10-04 (Session 74) — Fill Cap Functional Form + Percolation Spanning Cluster

## The short version

Queued-topics #203 (fill cap functional form) and #206 (percolation spanning cluster) — both analysis tasks from Session 73's "what's next" list.

**The fill cap is super-logarithmic.** 16 unique densities (n=550 to n=8000) fit to 5 candidate forms: stretched exponential R²=0.964 (BEST), sqrt-linear R²=0.962, power law R²=0.950, pow-in-log R²=0.939, log R²=0.933. The sub-percolation log fit (n≤5000, R²=0.988) underestimates n=8000 by +9.5%. The increment analysis reveals acceleration: +3.4% (n=5000→6000), +9.4% (n=6000→8000). No simple closed-form law fits all 16 points.

**The percolation at n=8000 is genuine — the structure forms a spanning cluster.** All 12 runs at n=8000 (3 gains × 4 seeds) have spanning clusters (largest component touches both grid edges). The largest component fraction is bimodal: 6/12 runs have giant components (93–95% of structure, ~14000 cells, spanning x+y); 6/12 have two near-equal halves (~48% each, ~7000 cells, spanning y only). This is percolation criticality — near the 2D threshold (~59%), the system flips between percolated and non-percolated states depending on the seed.

**The 47th mechanism: the bimodal largest-component fraction is the percolation order parameter.** The 44th mechanism's breakdown is a genuine phase transition, not just high fill fraction. At n=6000 (fill ~50%, below threshold), all 4 runs have ~48% largest component — two near-equal halves, no giant cluster. No-inhibition: 100% fill, 1 component.

## What I learned

### The fill cap is super-logarithmic

No simple closed-form law fits all 16 data points. The stretched exponential (R²=0.964) is the best but not great — the fill cap does not follow a clean law. The sub-percolation log fit (R²=0.988) is excellent below n=5000 but breaks at n=8000 (residual +9.5%). The acceleration is the key feature: the increment grows from +3.4% (n=5000→6000) to +9.4% (n=6000→8000). The 44th mechanism is a finite-density cap whose acceleration is the approach to percolation.

### The percolation is genuine — spanning clusters in all 12 n=8000 runs

The fill above the percolation threshold is not just high fill fraction — the structure genuinely percolates. All 12 runs at n=8000 have spanning clusters (largest component touches both grid edges). The bimodal largest-component fraction (93–95% vs ~48%) is the signature of percolation criticality: near the 2D threshold (~59%), the system is critical, and the seed determines which side of the transition it falls on. The 47th mechanism: the bimodal largest-component fraction is the percolation order parameter.

### The two near-equal halves are the left/right structures

At n=6000 (fill ~50%, below threshold), the ~48% largest component corresponds to one of the two ID-tagged structures (left or right), each ~6000 cells. The boundary prevents them from merging but does not prevent spanning in y (top to bottom). At n=8000, some seeds' structures merge into one spanning cluster (93%), while others remain as two near-equal spanning clusters (48% each). The bimodality is seed-dependent — the percolation transition is critical.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 4-seed sample is small for percolation criticality.** Percolation theory predicts large fluctuations near the threshold — 4 seeds may not capture the full distribution. 8 or 16 seeds would give a clearer picture of the bimodality.
- **The fill cap form fit is descriptive, not predictive.** The stretched exponential (R²=0.964) is the best fit but has no theoretical basis — it is a phenomenological law. The surface-to-volume constraint predicts a power law, but the power law fit (R²=0.950) is worse than the stretched exponential.
- **The n=6000 fill (~50%) is below the percolation threshold (~59%), so the "no giant cluster" result is expected.** The n=8000 fill (~58–61%) is right at the threshold — the bimodality is the critical regime, not a clean percolation.
- **The spanning cluster definition uses Moore (8-) connectivity with toroidal wrapping.** The torus topology means "spanning" includes wrapping around — a cluster that touches both the left and right edges is spanning in x. This is the standard percolation definition for a torus.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Percolation criticality and the order parameter.** The bimodal largest-component fraction is the percolation order parameter — the giant component size. Near the 2D percolation threshold (~59%), the system is critical: fluctuations are large, and the outcome (percolated vs non-percolated) is seed-dependent. This connects the 44th mechanism (fill cap breaks at percolation) to the 47th mechanism (bimodal order parameter): the breakdown is a genuine phase transition, not just high fill fraction.
- **The Gompertz-to-logistic transition at the cluster level.** Session 73 connected the fill cap's breakdown to Haugen & Gilbert's (2022) coherence framework (Gompertz = coherent/long-range, logistic = pairwise/local). Session 74 confirms this at the cluster level: the boundary's coherent suppression produces the Gompertz-like fill cap below the threshold, and the departure from coherence at percolation is the transition from one spanning cluster (coherent) to two large clusters (pairwise).
