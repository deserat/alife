# 2026-11-10 (Session 107) — The Capping→No-Capping Transition Is Between r/grid 4% and 5%

## The short version

Session 106 bracketed the sharp capping→no-capping transition to r/grid ∈ (3.33%, 5%). Session 107 tested 200×200 at r=8 (r/grid = 4%) to narrow it. Result: 200×200 is already in the no-capping regime — boundary gap < 1% at all n values, 0/4 stable at all fills (28–36%). The formula predicted ceiling = 36.9% but overestimates by at least 8.7pp (0/4 stable at fill = 28.2%). The 58th mechanism: the transition is bracketed to r/grid ∈ (4%, 5%) — a 1pp range, sharper than the 57th mechanism's 1.67pp bracket. The gap drops from ~56% (160×160, r/grid=5%) to <1% (200×200, r/grid=4%) — a 55pp drop over 1pp r/grid. The 55th mechanism (extensive over-splitting) is the cause: at 40000 cells the number of boundary-induced fragments exceeds the threshold for coherent suppression.

### Part 2 — The 58th mechanism: the transition is bracketed to r/grid ∈ (4%, 5%)

The boundary gap across grid sizes at r=8:

| Grid | r/grid | Boundary gap | Regime |
|------|--------|-------------|--------|
| 80×80 | 10% | ~large | Capping |
| 160×160 | 5% | ~56% | Capping |
| **200×200** | **4%** | **<1%** | **No-capping** |

The gap drops from ~56% to <1% over a 1pp change in r/grid (5% → 4%). The transition is sharper than the 57th mechanism's 1.67pp bracket: the actual range is 1pp. On 160×160 (25600 cells), the boundary's 8-cell dilation covers enough of the grid to cap fill coherently; on 200×200 (40000 cells), it does not. The 55th mechanism (extensive over-splitting) is the cause: at 40000 cells, the number of boundary-induced fragments (6–17 per region) exceeds the threshold for coherent suppression.
