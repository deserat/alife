# 2026-11-11 (Session 108) — The Capping→No-Capping Transition Is a Percolation-Like Topological Threshold on the Component Count

## The short version

The 59th mechanism: the capping→no-capping transition occurs at a critical **component count** (~5-6 per half-region), not a critical **fill fraction**. On 160×160 at the 30th ceiling (fill 43.5%, stable 0/4): mean_comp = 6.1. At the last stable point (fill 42.0%, stable 2/4): mean_comp = 4.2. On 200×200 at the lowest fill (fill 28.3%, stable 0/4): mean_comp = 8.3 — already above the 160×160 ceiling's 6.1, despite being 15pp lower in fill. The critical variable is topological (how many disconnected fragments can the boundary coherently suppress?), not geometric (what fraction of the grid is filled?). The percolation is of the boundary network, not the material. The 55th mechanism (extensive over-splitting) explains why the count scales with grid area: a larger grid at the same r/grid produces more fragments per region because the boundary's 8-cell dilation covers a smaller fraction of each region.

### Part 3 — The 59th mechanism: topological threshold on component count

The critical variable is the component count (~5-6 per region), not the fill fraction:
- 160×160 at fill 42.0% (stable 2/4): mean_comp = 4.2 → **below threshold → capping**
- 160×160 at fill 43.5% (stable 0/4): mean_comp = 6.1 → **at threshold → ceiling**
- 200×200 at fill 28.3% (stable 0/4): mean_comp = 8.3 → **above threshold → no-capping**

The transition occurs when the component count exceeds ~5-6 per region, regardless of fill fraction. The 55th mechanism (extensive over-splitting) explains why: a larger grid at the same r/grid produces more fragments per region because the boundary's 8-cell dilation covers a smaller fraction of each region. On 160×160 (12800 cells/region), the count reaches ~6 at the ceiling; on 200×200 (20000 cells/region), the count is > 8 even at the lowest fill — the larger area produces more fragments than the threshold at all densities.

The percolation analogy is refined from Session 106: the percolation is of the **boundary network** (can the suppression field coherently span the gap between the two structures?), not the **material network** (does the structure percolate?). The critical variable is the component count (topological — how many disconnected patches can the boundary coherently act through?), not the fill fraction (geometric — what fraction of the grid is occupied?). Standard 2D site percolation has a critical occupation fraction; our boundary percolation has a critical fragment count.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Boundary network percolation ↔ site percolation on the suppression field.** In standard 2D site percolation, the spanning cluster appears when the occupation fraction exceeds a critical value (~0.407 for Moore 8-neighbor). In our system, the connected capping boundary fragments when the component count per region exceeds ~5-6. This is a percolation of the suppression field's connectivity, not the material's. The critical variable is the count of fragments (topological), not the fill fraction (geometric). The distinction matters because the count scales with grid area (the 55th mechanism), making the transition grid-size-dependent — while a standard percolation threshold would be universal.
