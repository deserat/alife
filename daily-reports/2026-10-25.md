# 2026-10-25 (Session 94) — The 30th Ceiling Is NOT the Moore Threshold

## The short version

The 30th mechanism's persistence ceiling (~43.6% fill, where stable composition becomes impossible at any gain) was suspected to be the Moore 8-neighbor site percolation threshold (~40.7%). It is not. Compiling the fill-vs-stability data from 16 plateau sweep files (77 data points, 27 density levels, n=260–15000), the 30th ceiling is 2.93 percentage points above the Moore threshold — larger than the measurement noise (~0.2%). At the Moore threshold (fill ≈ 40.7%, n=1800), stability is still 2/4 — partially stable. The stability degrades gradually from ~35% fill and collapses sharply at ~43.6% (n=3000, stable 0/4 at all gains). The actual structure percolation (spanning cluster) happens at ~59% fill (n=8000, Session 74) — far above both. Three distinct thresholds: Moore (~40.7%, random site connectivity), 30th (~43.6%, boundary over-splitting), actual percolation (~59%, spanning cluster formation). The 30th is percolation-ADJACENT — the boundary's coherent suppression degrades starting near the Moore threshold — but it is not the percolation threshold itself. The 30th is the loss of coherent *stability*; the actual percolation is the loss of coherent *capping*.

## Topic

Queued-topics #3 from Session 93's "What's next": "Is the 30th mechanism's ceiling (~44%) the same as the Moore percolation threshold (~0.407)?" — the two numbers are close but not identical. Is the 30th a pre-threshold effect?

## What I did

Compiled the fill% and stability data from 16 plateau sweep JSON files (n=260 through n=15000 on 160×160, 77 data points across 27 density levels and 1–3 gains each). For each density level, extracted the best-gain stability (max stable_frac across gains) and the "all_zero" flag (whether stability is 0/4 at ALL gains). Identified the collapse point (first n where all_zero=True) and the last stable point (last n where stable_frac > 0). Compared both to the Moore 8-neighbor site percolation threshold (~0.407 = 40.7%).

## What I found

### The 30th ceiling is 2.93pp above the Moore threshold

- **Last n with any stability:** n=2500, fill=42.08% (1.38pp above Moore)
- **First n with ALL gains stable=0:** n=3000, fill=43.63% (2.93pp above Moore)
- **Moore threshold:** 40.7%
- **Actual structure percolation:** ~59% (n=8000, Session 74)

The gap (2.93pp) is larger than the measurement noise (~0.2% in fill from gain variation, ~1pp from 4-seed stability noise). The 30th ceiling is distinctly above the Moore threshold.

### At the Moore threshold, stability is still partially present

At fill ≈ 40.7% (n=1800), the best gain gives stable=2/4 (0.500). At fill ≈ 41.2% (n=2000), the best gain gives stable=3/4 (0.750). The stability degrades gradually from ~35% fill (where it first drops from 3/4 to 2/4 at some gains) and collapses sharply between 42% and 43.6%.

### Three distinct thresholds

1. **Moore (~40.7%):** random site connectivity threshold. At this fill, the structure's local connectivity starts to become long-range. Stability is still partially present.
2. **30th ceiling (~43.6%):** boundary over-splitting threshold. The boundary can no longer maintain STABLE composition at any gain. Each region has too many components (late_mean_lc, late_mean_rc > 3–5). This is a boundary-mechanism threshold, not a connectivity threshold.
3. **Actual percolation (~59%):** spanning cluster formation. The boundary-suppressed structure forms a connected spanning cluster (Session 74, percolation_spanning.py). The boundary delays percolation from ~40.7% to ~59%.

### The 30th is percolation-ADJACENT

The proximity (~3pp) is because the boundary's over-splitting becomes severe as the structures approach the Moore threshold — the local connectivity increase makes the boundary's coherent suppression less effective. But the stability doesn't collapse AT the threshold; it degrades gradually starting near the threshold and collapses a few percent above it. The boundary retains capping power above the Moore threshold (it delays the actual percolation to ~59%) but cannot maintain stable composition above ~43.6%.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Percolation theory → stigmergic stability:** The Moore threshold (~0.407) is the fill at which a random lattice becomes long-range connected. In our stigmergic system, this is where the boundary's coherent suppression starts to degrade — but the stability collapse happens a few percent above it, not at it. The 30th is a post-threshold boundary-mechanism effect, not the threshold itself.
- **Three-threshold phase diagram:** The Gompertz-to-logistic transition has three thresholds, not one: Moore (~40.7%, connectivity onset), 30th (~43.6%, stability collapse), actual percolation (~59%, spanning cluster). The 30th and the actual percolation are on different axes — the 30th is about the boundary's over-splitting capacity, the actual percolation is about the structure's spanning connectivity.
