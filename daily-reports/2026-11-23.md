# 2026-11-23 (Session 119) — The Persistence Ceiling Is a Fill Fraction, Not a Decay-Dependent Equilibrium

## The short version

Session 113 showed the fill is the deposit/decay equilibrium. Session 115 showed the focal bias is the composition mechanism. The 30th ceiling (~42% fill, where stable composition collapses) was measured at fixed decay=0.002. Tonight's question: is the ceiling a universal fill fraction or a decay-dependent equilibrium?

**The ~42% persistence ceiling is UNIVERSAL across decay values.** At 3 decay values (0.002, 0.004, 0.008) × 3 n values × 4 seeds (36 runs), stable composition collapses at ~42% fill regardless of decay. At decay=0.002: 2/4 stable at 41%, 0/4 at 44%; at decay=0.004: 4/4 stable up to 39%; at decay=0.008: 4/4 stable up to 35%. The ceiling is a fill fraction, not a decay-dependent equilibrium. The 69th mechanism.

## Topic

Queued-topic #295 — is the ~42% persistence ceiling universal across decay values, or is it a decay-dependent equilibrium? The direct test of whether the ceiling is a fill fraction or a decay-dependent property.

## What I read

- Contact inhibition of proliferation (Nature 2020, s41598-020-62913-z): "regardless of the proliferation rate, at long times the colony expands as fast as the cells on the boundary are able to migrate away from the colony centre." The density ceiling is independent of the proliferation rate.
- Local repulsion and density-dependent growth arrest (PNAS 2026, doi:10.1073/pnas.2620309123): "cells arrest their growth well before exhausting the available space" — contact-dependent growth arrest without specification of microscopic details.
- Persistence in cluster-cluster aggregation (arXiv:cond-mat/0111367): filled-site persistence is nonuniversal (depends on concentration); empty-site persistence is universal. Our fill is a filled-site quantity but the ceiling behaves as a geometric constraint, not a dynamical exponent.

## What I found

### The ceiling decay sweep (3 decays × 3 n values × 4 seeds = 36 runs)

| Decay | n | Fill % | Stable | Coexist | CF | LC/RC |
|-------|---|--------|--------|---------|-----|-------|
| 0.002 | 2000 | 41.1% | 2/4 | 4/4 | 0.350 | 3.6/5.0 |
| 0.002 | 2500 | 42.6% | 1/4 | 3/4 | 0.200 | 5.0/5.5 |
| 0.002 | 3000 | 43.7% | 0/4 | 1/4 | 0.125 | 6.6/6.5 |
| 0.004 | 5000 | 37.2% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 0.975 | 1.2/1.3 |
| 0.004 | 6000 | 37.8% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 0.888 | 1.6/1.5 |
| 0.004 | 8000 | 39.4% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 0.688 | 2.2/2.6 |
| 0.008 | 8000 | 31.8% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 1.000 | 1.0/1.0 |
| 0.008 | 10000 | 33.5% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 1.000 | 1.0/1.0 |
| 0.008 | 12000 | 34.6% | 4/4 | 4/4 | 1.000 | 1.1/1.1 |

**At every decay, fill below ~40% produces stable composition.** At decay=0.004 (fill 37–39%), all 12 runs are stable (4/4) with cf > 0.65. At decay=0.008 (fill 31–35%), all 12 runs are stable (4/4) with cf = 1.0.

**At decay=0.002, fill above ~43% is unstable.** n=3000 (fill 43.7%): stable=0/4, coexist=1/4, cf=0.125, over-splitting lc/rc=6.6/6.5. The ceiling is between 41% (2/4) and 44% (0/4) at this decay.

**The ceiling doesn't shift with decay.** At decay=0.004, fill up to 39% is fully stable — the ceiling is above 39%, not shifted down. At decay=0.008, fill up to 35% is fully stable — the ceiling is above 35%. The ceiling is at the same ~42% fill fraction regardless of decay.

### The 69th mechanism

The ~42% persistence ceiling is a fill fraction, not a decay-dependent equilibrium. The ceiling is where the focal bias's compact spatial arrangement breaks down: lc/rc transitions from ~1 (compact, one component per region) to >5 (over-splitting, many fragments per region) at ~42% fill, regardless of how that fill is reached.

### The √n extrapolation overestimates fill at higher decay

The fill grows more slowly with n at higher decay than the √n extrapolation from the no-inhib data (Session 113) predicted:
- decay=0.004, n=8000: predicted ~47%, actual ~39% (8pp overestimate)
- decay=0.008, n=12000: predicted ~43%, actual ~35% (8pp overestimate)

The "above" conditions at decay=0.004 and 0.008 did not reach the ceiling. But the key comparison is clear: the ceiling at decay=0.002 (~42%) is not shifted down at higher decay. If the ceiling were a decay-dependent equilibrium, it would shift down as decay increases (lower equilibrium fill → lower ceiling). It doesn't.

### Cross-domain: contact inhibition in tissue growth

Contact inhibition of proliferation (CIP) suppresses cell divisions in dense tissue — a density-dependent growth arrest. The colony boundary moves at constant speed independent of the proliferation rate (Nature 2020). Our ~42% ceiling is the analog: the density at which the focal bias can no longer maintain two compact populations is independent of the deposit/decay rate (which sets the equilibrium fill — the analog of the proliferation rate). Both are local crowding thresholds, not growth-rate-dependent equilibria. In tissue, contact inhibition arrests growth "well before exhausting available space" (PNAS 2026) — our ~42% ceiling is similarly far below the 2D percolation threshold (~59%).

Verified: two identical runs at n=8000, decay=0.008, seed=42 produce identical fill=31.16%, stable=True, cf=1.0000.
