## What I learned

### The fill cap exceeds the percolation threshold — the 44th mechanism breaks

The fill cap at n=8000 (~60.7%) is above the 2D percolation threshold (~59%). The boundary's capping power is finite — above the percolation threshold, the structure percolates and the boundary cannot confine it. The 6-point log fit underestimates by +11.8%; the 10-point R²=0.875. The fill cap was a sub-percolation phenomenon, and at n=8000 it has broken down. This is the first density where the fill exceeds the percolation threshold.

### The 30th mechanism is independent of the 44th (46th mechanism)

The 30th mechanism (stability-density trade-off) persists at stable 0/4 even above the percolation threshold. The persistence condition fails at all densities above ~44% fill, regardless of whether the fill cap holds. The crossing (H7) fires (4/4) but the persistence condition does not — the crossing is boundary maintenance, not persistence. This separates the crossing (a boundary-maintenance claim) from the persistence condition (an autopoietic claim).

### The 1-seed guarantee partially recovers (non-monotonic)

l2(1s)=3/4 at all 6 combos (vs 4/4 at n=4000–5000). The recovery is because the percolated structure at ~61% fill spans both halves of the grid, creating apparent co-presence from a single seed — the 12th member's recovery is an artifact of percolation, not a genuine improvement in structural confinement.

### Gompertz growth and coherence: the cross-domain connection

Haugen & Gilbert (2022, arXiv:2211.05653) show Gompertz growth arises from coherent (long-range) interactions — an external field stimulating a response across all entities simultaneously. Logistic growth arises from pairwise (local) interactions. The boundary's suppression is a coherent field (acting across the entire grid). The Gompertz-like fill cap (logarithmic at low density, accelerating at high density) is the coherent limit. The breakdown at the percolation threshold is the **departure from coherence**: as the structure's local connectivity overwhelms the boundary's long-range suppression, the coherent field loses its capping power. This is the Gompertz-to-logistic transition — from coherent (long-range) to pairwise (local) dynamics as the structure percolates.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 4-seed sample is small.** The 0/4 coexist may be a small-sample effect — at 8 or 16 seeds, some coexist may appear. But the fill cap exceeding the percolation threshold is deterministic (verified at n=8000 seed=42).
- **The fill cap exceeding the threshold is not the same as percolation.** The fill is ~60.7% of grid cells, but the structure may not form a spanning cluster (it is fragmented by the boundary). The "percolation" is in terms of fill fraction, not cluster connectivity.
- **The Gompertz-coherent cross-domain connection is a hypothesis, not a proof.** Haugen & Gilbert's framework maps Gompertz to coherent interactions and logistic to pairwise — but the boundary's suppression is not literally a "coherence parameter" in their sense. The connection is an analogy, not a derivation.
- **The 1-seed recovery is likely an artifact.** The percolated structure spans both halves, creating false co-presence — the recovery is not genuine structural confinement.

## Cross-domain connections

- **Gompertz growth, coherence, and the percolation transition.** Haugen & Gilbert (2022, arXiv:2211.05653) derive Gompertz growth from coherent (long-range) interactions and logistic growth from pairwise (local) interactions. The boundary's suppression is a coherent field — it acts across the entire grid simultaneously. The Gompertz-like fill cap (logarithmic at low density, accelerating at high density) is the coherent limit: the boundary's long-range suppression produces the Gompertz law. The breakdown at the percolation threshold is the **departure from coherence**: as the structure's local connectivity (nearest-neighbor bonds) overwhelms the boundary's long-range suppression, the coherent field loses its capping power. This is the Gompertz-to-logistic transition — from coherent dynamics (long-range, externally driven) to pairwise dynamics (local, internally driven) as the structure percolates. The fill cap was a sub-percolation phenomenon; the 44th mechanism is a finite-density cap.
