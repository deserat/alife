# 2026-11-20 (Session 117) — Compact Spatial Arrangement Is Specifically Required, Not Just Lower Fill

## The short version

Session 116 found the focal bias (exogenous + concentrating) produces perfect composition while region-restricted deposit (exogenous, diffuse) produces partial composition (cf=0.40) at fill 75.7% — above the ~44% ceiling. The confound: the focal bias had lower fill (34.5%). Tonight's matched-fill test sweeps decay for both conditions to compare at matched fill.

**At matched fill (focal 27.6% vs region 29.6%, 2pp apart): focal cf=1.0000, stable=4/4 vs region cf=0.0000, stable=0/4.** The focal bias produces perfect composition at every fill level (20.7%–34.5%); region-restricted produces zero composition at every fill level (8.5%–75.7%). The 67th mechanism: the focal bias's compact spatial arrangement (stepping toward a center → two compact structures, lc=rc=1.0) is specifically required — not just lower fill (region-restricted at lower fill still fragments, lc=25–32).

### The 67th mechanism

The focal bias creates two compact, well-separated structures (lc=rc=1.0 — one component per region). Region-restricted deposit creates diffuse, fragmented deposits (lc=25–32 — dozens of components per region). The curvature channel can't create enough local contrast with diffuse deposits — H7=0/4 for region-restricted at d≥0.008. The focal bias's compactness is what the curvature channel routes on.

### Cross-domain connections

**Instructive vs permissive exogenous signals (PMC8185431):** The focal bias is "instructive" (localized center → steering → compact deposits). Region-restricted is "permissive" (allows deposits in one half but doesn't steer → diffuse spread). Both are exogenous, but only the instructive form produces composition.

**Compact clustering vs diffusion-dominated fractal growth (arXiv:2504.16539):** The focal bias is the compact clustering regime (concentrated → nucleation → two compact structures). Region-restricted is the diffusion-dominated regime (diffuse → fractal fragmentation). The transition is about spatial arrangement, not fill level.
