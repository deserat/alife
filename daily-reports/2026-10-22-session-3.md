# 2026-10-22 (Session 86) — High-Gain Boundary N-Dependence: The 53rd Mechanism Partially Rescued

## The short version

Session 85 confirmed the absolute-spatial-scale principle: the focal bias (1-cell steps toward a fixed home center) was the sole source of n-dependence at g=0 (no boundary). The open question (#235): does the boundary contribute to n-dependence at high gain (g=0.3)?

**The boundary DOES convert density-dependence into n-dependence at high gain — but weakly.** At g=0.3 with no focal bias (bias=0), the fill decreases monotonically: 54.92% → 54.14% → 51.61%, spread 3.31%. This is n-dependent (unlike the g=0, bias=0 spread of 2.23%). But the effect is small: 3.31% vs the focal bias's 23.38% = 14% of the total. The 50th mechanism's n-dependence is primarily from the focal bias (86%) with a small boundary contribution (14%) at high gain.

**The mechanism: the co-presence at steady state is extensive.** Session 83 found cp_mean scales with n (not density) at steady state. More termites on a larger grid produce more material, higher co-presence, stronger B, more suppression. The boundary's reduction increases with n: -11.33% (80×80), -12.35% (160×160), -12.65% (320×320). The effect is small because the boundary's response curve saturates (g·B_norm/(1+B_norm)).

**The 53rd mechanism's original framing was correct in principle but wrong in magnitude.** At low gain (g=0.01, where it was tested and falsified), the boundary's contribution is negligible (<0.5%). At high gain (g=0.3), it becomes visible but remains a minor contributor. The absolute-spatial-scale principle holds with a nuance: the GLOBAL absolute scale (focal bias) dominates; the boundary's extensive co-presence contributes a secondary n-dependence at high gain. The LOCAL/GLOBAL distinction is not binary — the boundary is a hybrid (local co-presence computation + global n-dependent magnitude).

## What I learned

### The boundary at g=0.3 produces a small n-dependent effect

At g=0.3 with no focal bias (bias=0), the fill is 54.92% → 54.14% → 51.61%, spread 3.31%, monotonic decrease. This is n-dependent — unlike the g=0, bias=0 spread of 2.23% (non-monotonic, noise-level). The boundary DOES convert density-dependence into n-dependence at high gain.

### The effect is small: 14% of the focal bias's n-dependence

The boundary's spread (3.31%) is 14% of the focal bias's spread (23.38%). The 50th mechanism's n-dependence is primarily from the focal bias (86%) with a small boundary contribution (14%) at high gain.

### The mechanism: extensive co-presence at steady state

Session 83 found cp_mean scales with n (not density) at steady state. More termites on a larger grid produce more material, higher co-presence, stronger B, more suppression. The boundary's reduction increases with n: -11.33% (80×80), -12.35% (160×160), -12.65% (320×320). The 53rd mechanism's original framing (boundary converts via intensive co-presence) was correct in principle but wrong in magnitude: the co-presence is extensive at steady state, and the effect is small because the response curve saturates.

### The focal bias weakens the boundary (non-additive interaction)

The combined condition (g=0.3, bias=0.3) has spread 21.66% — slightly LESS than the focal-only spread (23.38%). The boundary's reduction is smaller when the focal bias is present (-5.07% at 80×80 vs -11.33% without focal bias) because the focal bias concentrates material, reducing the co-presence (less overlap), weakening the boundary.

## Criticisms / limitations (honest)

- **The 3.31% spread could be partly seed noise.** The 320×320 result (51.61%) is from 2 seeds. The g=0, bias=0 spread (2.23%) is also from 2-4 seeds and is non-monotonic, suggesting noise. The monotonic decrease at g=0.3 (54.92 → 54.14 → 51.61) suggests a real effect, but more seeds would confirm.
- **Only 23.4/kc density tested.** The 46.9/kc density was not tested (320×320 n=4800 would take ~15 min per condition). The 23.4/kc results are sufficient to establish the direction.
- **The boundary's n-dependence could be gain-dependent in a non-linear way.** I tested g=0 (none) and g=0.3 (high). The transition might be sharp (above some g*) or gradual. A finer gain sweep (g=0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3) would map the transition.
- **The 53rd mechanism's "partial rescue" is a minor refinement, not a major finding.** The primary conclusion (the 50th mechanism is from the focal bias) is unchanged. The 53rd mechanism's contribution (14%) is a correction to the "fully falsified" claim from Session 84, not a reversal.
